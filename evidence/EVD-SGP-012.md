# EVD-SGP-012 — Singapore's compute-access model: substantial domestic infrastructure combined with an explicitly acknowledged raw-compute ceiling and planned external dependence

## Evidence (from Source)

Singapore has more than 1.4 GW of data-centre capacity, AI research compute supported by the National Supercomputing Centre, and commercial cloud capacity, with additional data-centre capacity planned. At the same time, National AI Strategy 2.0 itself states that Singapore "needs reliable access to compute but cannot compete with major powers in assembling raw computing power" (MDDI, Launch of Singapore's Second National AI Strategy, 4 December 2023). A Parliamentary Question response (13 November 2024) sets out the government's own account of current and projected AI-specific compute and national cloud capacity. The government has committed up to S$500 million to secure high-performance compute resources, explicitly identifying leasing from external providers and partnerships with chipmakers and cloud-service providers as the anticipated mechanisms for securing that access, rather than exclusively domestic build-out.

## Constructs supported

- **S1 — Compute/infrastructure:** this evidence bears directly and specifically on Codebook §16's full S1 definition ("access to, dependence on, or constraints concerning compute, cloud services, models, chips, infrastructure, or related technological resources"), and distinguishes several of its sub-dimensions for Singapore:
  - *Domestic data-centre and infrastructure capacity:* substantial and expanding (>1.4 GW; planned additional capacity) — an enabling factor.
  - *Access to high-performance/frontier-relevant compute:* the government's own strategy document explicitly states Singapore "cannot compete with major powers in assembling raw computing power" — a direct, primary-source (E1/ES1) acknowledgment of a structural ceiling specifically on frontier-relevant compute, not a general economic-openness statement.
  - *Dependence on foreign GPUs/chips and reliance on commercial cloud/external providers:* the S$500 million compute-securing commitment is explicitly designed around leasing from external providers and partnerships with chipmakers and cloud-service providers — i.e., external reliance is the anticipated *mechanism* for meeting future high-performance compute needs, not a residual or incidental factor.
  - *Domestic technological/industrial capacity:* the National Supercomputing Centre and the S$500 million investment commitment demonstrate genuine, ongoing domestic capacity-building.
  - *Constraints on autonomous access to frontier compute:* explicitly acknowledged by the Singapore government itself, distinguishing this from a researcher inference.

This evidence, being primary-source (E1) and government-self-acknowledged (ES1), satisfies the specific evidentiary threshold articulated in the 2026-09-02 researcher decision on Singapore's earlier S1 item — namely, that a constraint must be *directly established* as concerning AI-specific compute/infrastructure, not general macroeconomic dependence, to be treated as S1-relevant. It supports **S1 — Mixed**: substantial domestic infrastructure capacity coexists with a government-acknowledged, structurally material constraint on autonomous frontier-compute capacity and a strategy that anticipates ongoing external reliance to meet future needs.
