# EVD-SGP-001 — NAIS strategic evolution (2019 → 2023 → 2026): deployment-first AI ecosystem and sectoral targeting

## Evidence (from Source)

Singapore's National AI Strategy evolved across three iterations. NAIS (2019) focused on deployment/adoption at national scale (five National AI Projects; nine priority sectors), explicitly framing the bottleneck as process/regulatory redesign rather than AI technology itself. NAIS 2.0 (2023) reframed this as "Opportunity → Necessity," "Local → Global," and "Projects → Systems," organised around 3 systems and 10 enablers (Activity Drivers; People & Communities; Infrastructure & Environment), with concrete targets (15,000 AI practitioners; more than S$500m committed via AI Singapore/RIE 2020–2025 plans; 70 AI Centres of Excellence; open-source regional models SEA-LION and MERaLiOn). The 2026 update refreshes the 10 enablers (e.g. "Placemaking" → "Ecosystem Integration") and announces four National AI Missions (Advanced Manufacturing, Financial Services, Connectivity, Healthcare) anchored by the newly formed National AI Council (chaired by PM Wong), plus SME-adoption programmes (National AI Impact Programme, 10,000 enterprises) and industry partnerships (NVIDIA research lab, Google and OpenAI national AI partnerships).

## Constructs supported

- **C1 — Technical expertise:** sustained multi-generational investment in AI research, talent (15,000-practitioner target, AI-bilingual talent), and compute/data infrastructure supports a Strong assessment, corroborated by [EVD-SGP-010](EVD-SGP-010.md).
- **C3 — Market resources:** the deliberate targeting of sectors already representing >40% of GDP (advanced manufacturing, financial services, connectivity, healthcare) for AI transformation, alongside a large existing base of AI-native businesses and startups, is direct evidence of substantial market resources, corroborated by [EVD-SGP-004](EVD-SGP-004.md) and [EVD-SGP-005](EVD-SGP-005.md).

## Notes

The three NAIS documents (2019, 2023, 2026 update) and the MDDI factsheet describing the same 2026 update are treated as **one evolving strategic trajectory**, not as four independent observations, since each iteration substantially restates and builds on its predecessor rather than introducing an unrelated fact. Singapore's own strategy explicitly self-characterises its focus as deployment/adoption rather than frontier AI R&D — this distinction is preserved rather than smoothed into a generic "Strong C1" reading.
