# EVD-IND-017 — Safe and Trusted AI Working Group Conclave, IIT Madras (10–11 Dec. 2025)

- **Source ID(s):** SRC-PIB-2025-003
- **Country/case:** CTY-IND
- **Episode ID:** EP-BLETCHLEY-SEQUENCE-2023
- **Source location:** PIB Chennai release, 11 Dec. 2025.

## Quotation / paraphrase

The Safe and Trusted AI Working Group's Conclave was hosted by IIT Madras' Centre for Responsible AI, bringing together "senior leaders from the government, industry, academia, civil society and global AI institutions." MeitY appointed Prof. Balaraman Ravindran (IIT Madras) as Working Group Chair — IIT Madras Director Prof. Kamakoti explicitly credits "MeitY for making my colleague, Prof. B. Ravindran as a co-chair." IndiaAI Mission Director Safirulla states each of the seven Working Groups "brings together experts from 112 countries and international organisations," and that "discussions here in Chennai... will feed directly into actionable, globally relevant outcomes for the Leaders' Declaration." The release explicitly situates this Conclave within the same lineage as "the UK AI Safety Summit, AI Seoul Summit, France AI Action Summit" — i.e., this Episode's own prior stages.

- **Relevant analytical code(s):** C4 (institutional/diplomatic resources — MeitY's appointment/administrative role in structuring the Working Group)
- **Evidence strength:** ES1 — Strong (E1, independently verified in full)
- **Coding status:** RAW

## Researcher notes

**Distinguishing India's own role from the Working Group's collective activity (per explicit instruction):** the *collective* activity — leaders from government, industry, academia, civil society and international AI institutions deliberating over two days — is a genuine multi-stakeholder consultative event, but the *event itself* was organised and hosted by IIT Madras (an Indian academic institution), not directly by the national government. **India's own documented organisational role is narrower and more specific: MeitY appointed the Working Group's chair.** This is a real administrative/coordinating act (selecting who would lead a specific international-facing consultative body), but it is an act of institutional design, not itself documented evidence of India negotiating, exchanging, or jointly producing output with the "112 countries and international organisations" the Working Group drew on. The claim that discussions "will feed directly into... the Leaders' Declaration" describes an intended output pathway, not a documented completed act of coordination. Per instruction, this is not coded as establishing M — see the Episode file's M reassessment.
