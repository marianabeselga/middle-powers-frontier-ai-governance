# EVD-SGP-005 — Digital economy scale, firm-level AI adoption, and regional AI-infrastructure leadership

## Evidence (from Source)

Singapore's digital economy reached S$128.1bn (18.6% of GDP) in 2024, up from 14.9% in 2019, with more than two-thirds of value added coming from non-ICT sectors (finance & insurance, wholesale trade, manufacturing). Firm-level AI adoption reached 14.5% of SMEs and 62.5% of non-SMEs in 2024, concentrated in information & communications, professional services, finance & insurance, and electronics. OECD's ASEAN Digital Trade Review identifies Singapore as the region's AI leader: US$9bn invested in AI-ready data centres, 7th globally in AI patents per 100,000 inhabitants (2023), and approximately 3.5 AI professionals per 1,000 workers — far above other ASEAN economies — while also noting Singapore remains substantially behind the US and China. OECD also highlights AI Singapore's role in firm-level AI-readiness assessment and advisory support, and describes Singapore's SME digitalisation framework (sector roadmaps, pre-approved solutions, grants, advisory services).

## Constructs supported

- **C3 — Market resources:** Singapore's C3 strength derives primarily from firm/investment/infrastructure **concentration and regional connectivity** rather than domestic market size alone — a highly digitalised economy, broad-based AI adoption, and clear regional leadership in AI investment and patents — supporting **C3 — C-3 (Strong)**, corroborated by [EVD-SGP-001](EVD-SGP-001.md) and [EVD-SGP-004](EVD-SGP-004.md).
- **S1 — Compute/infrastructure:** substantial AI-ready data-centre investment and a comparatively deep AI-professional talent pool support an enabling reading of Singapore's AI-specific infrastructure conditions. See [EVD-SGP-011](EVD-SGP-011.md) for a genuinely distinct macro-economic dependence dimension that creates an unresolved tension with this reading.
- **S2 — Market size/dependence:** a highly digitalised, sector-diverse economy provides favourable conditions for AI deployment and coordination; Singapore's relatively small absolute population/market size means the evidence does not establish substantial *external* dependence on access to the Singaporean market specifically — supporting **S2 — Enabling** rather than an unqualified "Strong" reading.
