# EVD-IND-006 — India AI Governance Guidelines and regulatory/institutional landscape

## Quotation / paraphrase

**Governance Guidelines:** drafted by a MeitY-constituted committee (from July 2025), structured in four parts around **seven "sutras"** (Trust is the Foundation; People First; Innovation over Restraint; Fairness and Equity; Accountability; Understandable by Design; Safety, Resilience and Sustainability) and **six recommendation pillars** (Infrastructure; Capacity Building; Policy & Regulation; Risk Mitigation; Accountability; Institutions), plus a phased Action Plan (short/medium/long-term) and practical guidelines for industry/regulators. Recommends new institutions: an AI Governance Group (AIGG), a Technology & Policy Expert Committee (TPEC), and confirms the AI Safety Institute (AISI)'s standards/research role. Explicitly frames itself as building on existing law (IT Act 2000, IT Rules 2021, DPDP Act 2023) rather than creating a single comprehensive AI Act, and flags a possible future "Digital India Act" as a medium/long-term legislative option, alongside piloting regulatory sandboxes.

## Researcher notes

Researcher's provisional assessment: C2 = C-2 Moderate; S4 = Enabling. Researcher rationale: "meaningful regulatory and governance capacity, but the evidence provided does not establish mature implementation and enforcement capacity across AI governance. A Moderate assessment is therefore more appropriate than Strong" (C2); "these arrangements provide an enabling institutional environment for coordination, although the evidence does not necessarily demonstrate uniformly mature implementation capacity" (S4).

**Law-in-force vs. guideline/policy distinction (preserved explicitly):** of the 11 tracked instruments, only the IT Rules 2021 is squarely a binding "Regulation" already in force; the DPDP Act 2023 (a binding "Act") is "Awaiting Entry" — not yet in force; the AI Governance Guidelines and FREE-AI Framework are non-binding "Guidelines," despite being marked "In Force" by the tracker (i.e., currently operative as guidance, not as enacted law); the remaining items are policies/approach papers/summit outputs at various stages (Adopted, Draft, Under Review). No instrument here is treated as a comprehensive horizontal "AI Act" — the Governance Guidelines note itself states "India does not have a single 'AI Act.'" The prospective Digital India Act is recorded as a discussed future legislative option, not existing law.
