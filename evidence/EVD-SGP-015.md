# EVD-SGP-015 — Paris announcements: Global AI Assurance Pilot, Singapore–Japan Joint Testing Report, Red Teaming Challenge (Feb. 2025)

- **Source ID(s):** SRC-IMDA-2025-003 (existing Stage 1 source — independently re-fetched and re-verified in full for this Episode, 2026-09-04); SRC-ELYSEE-2025-001 (existing, Paris Statement)

- **Episode ID:** EP-BLETCHLEY-SEQUENCE-2023
- **Source location:** IMDA/MDDI joint press release, 11 Feb. 2025

## Quotation / paraphrase

At the Paris AI Action Summit, Minister Josephine Teo announced three initiatives: (1) the **Global AI Assurance Pilot** (AI Verify Foundation/IMDA), a testbed for global best practices in GenAI technical testing; (2) a **Joint Testing Report with Japan**, conducted as co-lead (with Japan) of the Testing and Evaluation Track under the AI Safety Institute (AISI) Network — "global linguistic and technical experts from the AISI network" tested LLM guardrails across **10 languages** (Cantonese, English, Farsi, French, Japanese, Kiswahili, Korean, Malay, Mandarin, Telugu) and **five harm categories** (violent crime, non-violent crime, IP, privacy, jailbreaking); (3) the **Singapore AI Safety Red Teaming Challenge Evaluation Report 2025**, based on a Nov. 2024 exercise with 350+ participants from nine Asia-Pacific countries red-teaming four LLMs. All three were explicitly framed as building on Bletchley and Seoul.

- **Relevant analytical code(s):** P1 (position); C4 (technical/institutional capability mobilised); M1/M2/M3b (**established** — Singapore and Japan actually conducted a completed joint testing exercise, not a planned or prospective one; this is the single clearest completed-and-documented bilateral technical coordination found across any of the six cases in this Episode); I3/I5 (standards/joint-evaluation instruments — the Pilot and the Joint Testing Report); L3 (evaluation/testing — direct); GL1/GL2/GL3 (candidate — see Researcher notes on attribution)

It is formalized here as well because it was specifically *announced as a Paris-stage deliverable*, at one of this Episode's own Table 2 events, with a concrete, dated, completed output (not the Network's abstract creation, which remains properly analysed under EP-AISI-NETWORK-2024). This distinction — a completed, country-specific technical output delivered at a Table 2 event, vs. the Network's own multilateral founding — is the basis for treating this specific fact as in-scope here. Flagged for researcher confirmation, since it necessarily touches the same underlying coordination architecture as the separately-registered episode.

**GL attribution:** the Global AI Assurance Pilot and the Red Teaming Challenge are Singapore's own unilateral initiatives (not multi-case coordination) — real institutional/information-production consequences, but not "governance leverage" arising from coordination *with another of the six cases* in the sense §15 intends for this comparative study; they are retained as GL evidence for Singapore's own governance-infrastructure role, consistent with Decision Rule 5 (capability + mechanism may both be coded where mobilisation is evidenced).
