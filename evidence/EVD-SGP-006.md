# EVD-SGP-006 — IEP: Singapore as a distinct "emerging power," ASEAN bilateral influence

## Evidence (from Source)

The IEP's "The Great Fragmentation" report characterises Singapore as "an emerging power (different from others), not a middle or major power," with increasing bilateral power/influence in other ASEAN countries over 1991–2023.

## Constructs supported

- **C4 — Institutional/diplomatic resources:** growing bilateral influence within ASEAN corroborates the international-engagement evidence in [EVD-SGP-007](EVD-SGP-007.md), [EVD-SGP-008](EVD-SGP-008.md), and [EVD-SGP-009](EVD-SGP-009.md).
- **S3 — Geopolitical alignments:** an expanding regional-influence position supports **S3 — Enabling**.

## Notes

**Fourth distinct tier classification (not reinterpreted):** across the six cases processed, this same IEP report has now applied four different classifications — "great power"/"greater power" (India, Japan), "(rising) middle power" (Brazil, Canada, Korea), and now, for Singapore, an explicitly separate **"emerging power"** category, stated by the report itself to be neither a middle power nor a major power. Consistent with the policy applied in all five prior cases, this classification is **not reinterpreted, altered, or treated as an error** — it is the IEP's own taxonomy, flagged for researcher awareness as part of the broader case-selection/theoretical question the project's "AI middle power" framing must eventually address, not a Stage 1 coding issue. This point is carried into the six-country consistency review.
