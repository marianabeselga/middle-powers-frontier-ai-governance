# EVD-CAN-003 — Sovereign AI compute and infrastructure

- **Source ID(s):** SRC-ISED-2026-002 (AI for All, Pillar 4 and "Sovereign compute, cloud, and connectivity"); SRC-ISED-2024-001 (Canadian Sovereign AI Compute Strategy)

- **Country/case:** CTY-CAN
- **Episode ID:** —
- **Source location:** AI for All, Pillar 4 ("Building the Canadian sovereign AI foundation") and "Sovereign compute, cloud, and connectivity"; Canadian Sovereign AI Compute Strategy, "Mobilizing private sector investment" and "Building public supercomputing infrastructure"

## Quotation / paraphrase

**Constraining (foreign dependence):** "Canada will require 5.5 gigawatts (GW) worth of AI compute for its commercial players by 2030... Canada's current AI data centre and cloud offerings are largely foreign-owned and controlled"; Canada "relies on global fabrication capacity for GPUs" (chip fabrication almost entirely offshore).

**Enabling (sovereign investment):** Budget 2024 announced "$2 billion over five years... to launch new initiatives" including the AI Compute Challenge (up to $700M to expand commercial AI-specific data-centre capacity) and the AI Sovereign Compute Infrastructure Program/SCIP (up to $1 billion for a Canadian-owned public supercomputer), plus up to $200M near-term augmentation of existing public compute and an AI Compute Access Fund (up to $300M). AI for All commits to building "a world-leading public supercomputer" and to private-sector partnerships proposing "850 MW of compute capacity by 2030, with scaling capacity of up to 2.3 GW"; also references enhancing chip design/fabrication "building on the recent announcement to spin off the National Research Council's Photonics Fabrication Centre." Canada's electricity grid is described as "sustainable and predictable, but currently constrained," with a National Electricity Strategy targeting a doubling of electricity infrastructure by 2050.
