# EVD-CAN-018 — Canada's founding/institutional role and expert participation in GPAI

- **Source ID(s):** SRC-OECDAI-2025-001 (independently verified — RAI WG); SRC-OECDAI-2024-001 (independently verified — Innovation & Commercialisation WG, specialist tier)
- **Country/case:** CTY-CAN
- **Episode ID:** EP-GPAI-2024
- **Source location:** RAI Working Group report (member count by country); Innovation & Commercialisation WG roster

## Quotation / paraphrase

Confirmed: Canada has 2 members in the Responsible AI Working Group (of 30 total, 17 countries) and a "Specialist"-tier contributor (Marc-André Sirard, Université Laval) to the Innovation & Commercialisation Working Group. Combined with the researcher's cited (not independently re-fetched) founding-member status, hosting of the first GPAI Multistakeholder Experts Group Plenary (Montréal, Dec. 2020), and establishment of CEIMIA as a GPAI Expert Support Centre, Canada shows both institutional/leadership-level contribution and expert-level Working Group participation.

- **Relevant analytical code(s):** C1 (technical, via named experts); C2 (regulatory/institutional, via CEIMIA); C4 (institutional/diplomatic — founding architect role, hosting); M1 (coalition-building — founding/institution-building act, established); M2 (joint production — established at the Working Group level)
- **Evidence strength:** ES1 — Strong (E2, independently verified for WG membership; founding/CEIMIA claims not independently re-fetched but consistent with well-documented public facts)
- **Coding status:** RAW

## Researcher notes

Canada's contribution is properly separated into two distinct tiers: (1) **institutional/founding-level** — genuinely state-attributable (Canada co-founded GPAI, hosted its first Plenary, established and continues to fund CEIMIA as a national Expert Support Centre) — this is a direct, documented state act, not mediated through independent experts; (2) **expert-participation level** — Canada nominated experts who then act independently within Working Groups (per `SRC-OECDAI-2025-001`'s explicit independence qualification), a weaker form of state attribution. Both are recorded, kept distinct.
