# EVD-KOR-001 — National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence (2019): industrial base and strategic bets

## Evidence (from Source)

The 2019 National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence organises Korea's AI policy around three pillars (AI ecosystem, AI utilisation, people-centred AI) and nine strategies/100 action tasks. It explicitly builds on Korea's existing structural strengths — semiconductors, manufacturing, ICT/network infrastructure, large corporate groups, high educational attainment, strong digital-government infrastructure — rather than attempting to replicate US/Chinese frontier-AI approaches, with a particular strategic bet on combining "AI + semiconductors + manufacturing." It identifies AI talent as a strategic bottleneck and proposes a talent-development hierarchy (top researchers → AI professionals → AI practitioners → AI-literate population).

## Constructs supported

- **C1 — Technical expertise:** sustained national investment in AI R&D, talent pipelines (university programmes, industry-linked training, interdisciplinary education), and AI semiconductor/PIM technology development supports a Strong assessment.
- **C3 — Market resources:** the strategy's deliberate leveraging of Korea's existing large industrial base (semiconductors, manufacturing, ICT, large corporate groups) as the foundation for AI-market development is direct evidence of substantial market resources, corroborated by [EVD-KOR-004](EVD-KOR-004.md) and [EVD-KOR-005](EVD-KOR-005.md).
- **S1 — Compute/infrastructure (added 2026-09-02, S1 calibration):** the strategy's explicit identification of "AI semiconductors/PIM and next-generation AI technologies as areas where Korea could potentially establish global leadership" is direct, primary-source (E1/ES1) evidence bearing on S1's "domestic infrastructure and compute capacity" and "access to frontier-relevant compute" sub-dimensions — it documents Korea's own strategic positioning of its semiconductor-manufacturing base (memory and, increasingly, logic/foundry capacity) as an upstream *supplier* within the global AI-hardware production chain, not merely a downstream consumer of foreign chips. This is a materially different structural starting point than a country whose AI-hardware access rests solely on imports, and is used together with [EVD-KOR-005](EVD-KOR-005.md) in the S1 assessment in [CTY-KOR.md](../00-Countries/CTY-KOR.md).

## Notes

The strategy itself identifies its own gaps: weaker development on SME/traditional-sector AI adoption and measurable productivity gains, cross-ministerial coordination/execution/diffusion mechanisms, retention of international AI talent, and a pathway from domestic capability to globally competitive AI exports. These self-identified gaps are preserved as researcher-observation context, not smoothed over in the C1/C3 assessment.
