## Quotation / paraphrase

**Original basis (Regulations.ai, 2026-08-30):** Nine named instruments: Resolução CNJ nº 615/2025; Portaria CNJ nº 270/2025; Decreto nº 12.308/2024; Resolução CITDigital nº 1/2025; Resolução CITDigital nº 2/2025; Portaria nº 8/2025; Estratégia Brasileira de Inteligência Artificial (EBIA, 2021); Portaria MCTI nº 4.979/2021; Projeto de Lei nº 2.338/2023 (Marco Legal da IA). **Expanded basis — legislative trajectory (2026-08-30, controlled revision):** PL 2.338/2023 "propõe 'general national rules for the development, implementation and responsible use' of AI systems"; approved by the Senate in December 2024; under consideration in the Chamber of Deputies. PL 6.237/2025 proposes a National System for AI Development, Regulation and Governance (Sistema Nacional para Desenvolvimento, Regulação e Governança de Inteligência Artificial, SIA), "designed to coordinate regulatory, supervisory and sanctioning powers," including a Brazilian Council for Artificial Intelligence (CBIA) described in the bill as the "órgão máximo de formulação, coordenação e supervisão da política nacional de inteligência artificial" ["highest body for the formulation, coordination and supervision of national AI policy"]. In March 2026, PL 6.237/2025 was attached to PL 2.338/2023. The bill also references intent to "fomentar o desenvolvimento de plataformas digitais, serviços de computação em nuvem, modelos e aplicações de inteligência artificial no País."

## Construct-specific justification (C2 vs. S4)

- **C2 — Regulatory expertise/capacity:** the PBIA, EBIA, and the legislative process itself (a bill passing the Senate, an Executive proposal for a dedicated AI governance system and council, active inter-institutional coordination between Senate, Chamber, and Executive) are evidence of Brazil's **capacity to formulate national AI policy and regulation** — i.e., regulatory/policy-making capacity — not evidence that a binding AI law is in force. Capacity and enactment are kept distinct per Codebook §8 (capability ≠ mobilisation/outcome).
- **S4 — Regulatory/institutional arrangements:** the same instruments, plus existing sector-specific regulation (CNJ Resolution 615/2025) and the LGPD, describe the **current regulatory and institutional architecture** conditioning AI governance in Brazil — an architecture that combines soft-law strategy, sectoral hard law, and pending horizontal legislation, rather than a single, settled regulatory regime.

## Classification of instrument status (do not conflate)

- **Policy/strategy, soft-law (non-binding):** EBIA (2021); PBIA (2024–2028). These are strategic policy documents, not legislation.
- **Sectoral hard law / binding regulation currently in force:** LGPD (data protection); Resolução CNJ nº 615/2025 (Judiciary-specific).
- **Proposed legislation, not yet in force:** PL 2.338/2023 (Senate-approved, December 2024; under Chamber consideration) and PL 6.237/2025 (attached to PL 2.338/2023, March 2026). Neither constitutes AI legislation currently in force in Brazil. The absence of a comprehensive horizontal AI law in force is not evidence of an absence of AI governance — it coexists with an active, multi-institutional policy and legislative architecture.
