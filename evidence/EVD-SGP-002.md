# EVD-SGP-002 — Singapore's AI regulatory/institutional landscape: principles-based governance, no comprehensive binding AI statute

## Evidence (from Source)

Singapore's AI-specific governance layer is principles-based and voluntary: the Model AI Governance Framework (first launched 2019, updated for Agentic AI in 2026), AI Verify (launched 2022, open-sourced 2023 — see [EVD-SGP-003](EVD-SGP-003.md)), and sector-specific guidance (PDPC advisory guidelines on AI/personal data; MAS FEAT principles and AI model-risk-management observations; a Singapore Courts guide on generative AI use by court users). Of the 13 instruments tracked by Regulations.ai, only the Elections (Integrity of Online Advertising) (Amendment) Act 2024 is a binding Act with AI/deepfake-adjacent provisions; the Workplace Fairness Bill (passed in Parliament in 2025 per its own description, though still tracked as Type="Bill") and the Online Safety (Relief and Accountability) Bill (Under Review, 2025) are further binding or near-binding instruments addressing AI-adjacent harms, alongside the pre-existing Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), which governs AI-related personal-data processing. Multiple institutions share AI-governance responsibilities: IMDA (Model AI Governance Framework, industry development), PDPC (PDPA enforcement, AI-privacy guidance), MAS (financial-sector AI supervision), MOM (Workplace Fairness Act), the Elections Department, the Singapore Courts, AI Singapore, and the Smart Nation and Digital Government Office (SNDGO, overall strategy coordination).

## Constructs supported

- **C2 — Regulatory expertise/capacity:** Singapore's Strong C2 rating rests on a **materially different basis** than Japan's or Korea's: rather than a comprehensive binding horizontal AI statute, it rests on (a) a mature multi-agency institutional architecture, (b) a widely-adopted voluntary testing/assurance toolkit (AI Verify), and (c) targeted binding sectoral legislation addressing specific AI-adjacent harms (election deepfakes, workplace discrimination, data protection) rather than AI as such. This distinction is preserved explicitly rather than presented as equivalent regulatory comprehensiveness to Japan's Act No. 53 or Korea's AI Basic Act.
- **S4 — Regulatory/institutional arrangements:** the combination of principles-based frameworks, sector-specific institutions, and public-private assurance mechanisms (AI Verify, Global AI Assurance Pilot — see [EVD-SGP-009](EVD-SGP-009.md)) provides multiple institutional channels for AI governance coordination, supporting **S4 — Enabling**.
