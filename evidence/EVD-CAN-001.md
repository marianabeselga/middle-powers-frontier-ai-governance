# EVD-CAN-001 — Pan-Canadian AI Strategy: research/talent institutions and standards pillar

- **Episode ID:** —

## Quotation / paraphrase

The Pan-Canadian Artificial Intelligence Strategy launched in 2017 "to strengthen Canada's leadership in AI research and expand the country's pool of skilled experts." Phase I (2017–2022, $125M via Budget 2017) established the Canada CIFAR AI Chairs program and the three National AI Institutes — Amii, Mila, and the Vector Institute — with CIFAR leading national research/training/collaboration programs. Phase II (2021–2031, $443.8M via Budget 2021 plus $174M via the 2024 Fall Economic Statement) added three pillars: Commercialization, Standards (Standards Council of Canada advancing AI-related standards), and Talent and Research. Supporting programs: the National AI Institutes ($60M Budget 2021 + $24M 2024 FES), Canada's Global Innovation Clusters ($125M + $150M), the Standards Council of Canada AI work ($8.6M), CIFAR programs ($208M over 10 years), and the Digital Research Alliance of Canada ($40M).

Separately, per AI for All: "The foundational ideas behind today's systems trace through Canadian researchers: Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton, and Turing Award winners Yoshua Bengio and Richard Sutton, the three so-called godfathers of AI... Canada is home to one of only a handful of frontier model companies in the world."
