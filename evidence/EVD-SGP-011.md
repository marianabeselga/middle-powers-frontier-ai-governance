# EVD-SGP-011 — IMF Article IV Consultation 2026: macro-economic and energy external dependence, offset by substantial financial buffers

## Evidence (from Source)

The IMF's 2026 Article IV Consultation documents Singapore's deep integration into the global economy: goods exports at 107.9% of GDP and goods imports at 78.7% of GDP in 2025, and energy import dependence (more than 90% of electricity generated from imported natural gas; net oil/gas imports ~7% of GDP). The IMF notes Singapore's export-oriented economy is vulnerable to global trade tensions, technology-supply-chain disruption, and a potential reversal of the global AI boom. At the same time, Singapore holds substantial buffers: a current-account surplus of 16.7% of GDP, a net international investment position of 179.1% of GDP, and official FX reserves of approximately US$409bn (68% of GDP) — assessed by the IMF as leaving Singapore's external position "substantially stronger" than implied by medium-term fundamentals.

## Constructs supported

- **S1 — Compute/infrastructure:** this evidence documents **general macro-economic and energy external dependence** (trade openness, imported-energy reliance for electricity generation) rather than AI-specific hardware/compute/chip dependence. The Research Note's own assessment reads this as **S1 — Mixed** ("structurally externally dependent while comparatively resilient"). This is a genuinely different evidentiary basis than the AI-specific compute/data-centre-investment evidence in [EVD-SGP-005](EVD-SGP-005.md), which independently supports S1 — Enabling across two notes. **Resolved by researcher decision, 2026-09-02 (see Notes): this evidence is retained as contextual/background evidence and does not convert S1 to Mixed.**

## Notes

**Original flag (2026-09-01):** this evidence was initially flagged as creating a cross-note conflict on S1 — "Unresolved — researcher decision required" in [CTY-SGP.md](../00-Countries/CTY-SGP.md) — because, unlike the Brazil S4 or Singapore C4 precedents (where new evidence reinforced or extended the *same underlying fact* already reflected in an existing assessment), the IMF evidence introduced a **materially different type of evidence** (macro-economic/energy dependence, not AI-hardware/compute/chip/cloud dependence) not addressed by the notes supporting S1 = Enabling, reaching an explicitly different value on the Codebook's own S-scale. Per Codebook §8 and Decision Rule 12, this was treated as a genuine construct-level conflict requiring researcher judgment, analogous to the CTY-CAN C3 precedent, rather than resolved by the assistant.

**Researcher decision (2026-09-02), following the controlled Stage 1 consistency review:** per Codebook §16, S1 is defined as "access to, dependence on, or constraints concerning compute, cloud services, models, chips, infrastructure, or related technological resources." General macroeconomic/energy-import dependence is not treated as S1 unless the evidence directly establishes a constraint on AI-specific compute, cloud, chips, data-centre capacity, or related technological infrastructure. This evidence's own text (trade-to-GDP ratios; electricity generation from imported natural gas) does not directly establish such a constraint — the IMF source itself does not tie the energy dependence to AI compute or data-centre capacity specifically. Accordingly, this evidence is **retained as contextual/background evidence** on Singapore's broader external-dependence profile, but is **not used to alter the S1 rating**, which is governed by [EVD-SGP-005](EVD-SGP-005.md). This is a researcher decision on the proper scope of S1 under the Codebook, not a mechanical pick between the two candidate values, and not a new cross-case threshold rule.
