# Stage 5B — Network Analysis Memo

## Analytical principle, restated

Network analysis is secondary to, and derivative of, the process-tracing findings already established in the four frozen Episodes and reviewed in `03-Episodes/Stage4-Cross-Case-Matrix.md`. Nothing in this memo establishes or re-establishes M, L, or GL; nothing here is used to infer causal mechanisms, substantive influence, or power. The question this stage asks is narrower: given the relational structures the process tracing already found, does representing them as a network add useful comparative information, and are the patterns visible in the network consistent with what the process tracing already concluded?

## Network definitions

Three representations were built, each answering a different question and each kept structurally distinct rather than collapsed into one graph:

1. **Bipartite participation/access network** — actors and Episodes as two node types; an edge records documented participation, not coordination. Built from Dataset 1, collapsing its 47 role-level rows to one tie per actor–Episode pair actually present.
2. **Direct actor-to-actor established coordination network** — only the 2 relationships in Dataset 2's Table 2A, where exactly two named actors are documented in a joint activity (Singapore–Japan, Bletchley; Japan–India, Hiroshima). This is the only representation in which an edge can be read as a genuine dyadic tie between two states.
3. **Actor-to-configuration (hub) network** — the 22 relationships in Dataset 2's Table 2B, where established M involves either 3+ actors jointly attached to one named mechanism/coalition/instrument, or a single actor unilaterally attached to one it constructed or chairs. This is bipartite (actor side, hub side), not actor-to-actor.

## Metric choices, and why others were not calculated

For representation 2 (the only one where actor-to-actor metrics are meaningful at all), degree, betweenness centrality, density, and connected components were calculated, exactly as the task specified, with two metrics explicitly *not* calculated: **weighted degree** (no validated weight variable exists — `Edges.md`'s `edge_weight` column was proposed and declined by the researcher on 2026-08-30, per `06-Relational-Data/README.md`, and this stage does not revisit that decision) and **network-wide betweenness as a ranking device** (with only 2 edges in the entire graph, a single non-zero betweenness value is a structural artefact of a 3-node path, not a comparative finding — reported once, in the metrics table, with that caveat attached, and not used again).

For representation 1 (bipartite participation), ordinary one-mode density/centrality measures were not applied, since a one-mode projection of "who shares a forum with whom" would immediately reintroduce the participation-as-coordination conflation this entire Stage 5 effort exists to prevent. Bipartite-appropriate measures — actor breadth (how many Episodes an actor appears in) and Episode breadth (how many actors an Episode reaches) — were used instead.

For representation 3 (actor-to-configuration), the same logic applies with additional force: this graph's whole reason for existing is to avoid inventing actor-to-actor ties from shared hub membership, so no one-mode projection was computed, and no betweenness was calculated on the hub side (which would conflate "attached to many configurations" with "brokers between actors" — a claim this stage does not make). Affiliation counts (bipartite degree) were used instead, exactly as instructed.

## Treatment of hubs

Every hub node (MECHANISM, COALITION, or INSTRUMENT type, per Codebook §24) represents either (a) a genuinely multi-actor configuration where the underlying evidence documents joint contribution to one named process without specifying every pairwise exchange, or (b) a unilateral institutional-entrepreneurship act with only one or two actors attached and no documented tie *between* co-attached actors. These two cases were not collapsed: `Stage5B-Network-Metrics-Dataset.md` §3 explicitly distinguishes the GPAI Founding Coalition hub (2 actors attached, Canada and Japan, individually established, with no documented direct Canada–Japan tie) from the two true singleton-attachment hubs (Japan alone on the Hiroshima Friends Group; India alone on the GPAI–OECD Integrated Partnership). Hub degree is reported as an affiliation count, never as a proxy for the hub's own importance or governance effect — the hub nodes carry no GL, L, or influence value of any kind in this representation.

## Treatment of expert/state distinctions

Every actor→hub edge in the GPAI Working Group hubs is tagged coordination level **expert/epistemic**, reflecting the frozen Episode's own explicit statement that Working Group members "act with full independence" of their governments. This is never merged with the state/diplomatic-level edges to the GPAI Founding Coalition or GPAI–OECD Integrated Partnership hubs, even where the same country (e.g., Canada, Japan) has both kinds of tie. No metric in this stage sums or averages a country's expert-tier and state-tier affiliation counts into one figure that would obscure this distinction — the affiliation-count table in the metrics dataset reports a total, but the accompanying per-hub table always allows that total to be decomposed back into its expert/state components.

## Key structural findings

**Participation is substantially broader than established coordination, but unevenly so across Episodes.** Bipartite participation density is 0.875–0.917; the direct actor-to-actor coordination graph has a density of 0.133. But the gap is not uniform: in the AISI Network, every one of the 4 participating actors also has an established coordination tie (a 0-actor gap); in GPAI, all 6 participating actors have at least one established tie, though of sharply uneven type and count (a 0-actor gap at the binary level, but a wide gap in tie *strength*); in Bletchley, 2 of 6 participating actors (Brazil, Korea) show no established coordination at all; in Hiroshima, 4 of 6 (Brazil, Canada, Korea, Singapore) show none — the widest gap of the four Episodes. This is a genuinely new comparative observation the network representation adds: the "participation ≫ coordination" pattern already anticipated in the Cross-Case Matrix is confirmed in aggregate, but it is not one pattern — it is at least two distinct patterns (a near-total conversion of participation into some form of established tie, in AISI Network and GPAI; a narrow, selective conversion, in Bletchley and especially Hiroshima).

**The Matrix's "selective sub-coalition inside a broad forum" pattern is confirmed for Bletchley and Hiroshima, and qualified for GPAI.** In Bletchley, only 4 of 6 participating actors reach any established coordination — a genuine subset. In Hiroshima, only 2 of 6 do — the clearest case of a small named group (Japan, India) standing apart from a much larger nominal membership (the Friends Group's 66 countries by 2026, and the other four study cases within it). In GPAI, however, the network shows something structurally different: *all six* study cases reach at least one established coordination tie — there is no excluded subset at the level of "any tie at all." What *is* selective in GPAI is the tier: only 3 of 6 (Canada, Japan, India) reach a state/diplomatic-level founding or chairmanship tie, while all 6 reach the expert/epistemic Working Group tier. The Cross-Case Matrix's own §8 already recorded the founding-coalition and Working-Group rows as separate configurations, so this is not a contradiction of the Matrix — but the network representation sharpens the point: GPAI's "selective sub-coalition" operates on tie *type*, not on binary inclusion, and grouping it with Bletchley's and Hiroshima's actor-level selectivity in one undifferentiated sentence (as a first reading of the Matrix's §3 bullet might suggest) would overstate the similarity. AISI Network remains the clean exception, exactly as the Matrix already noted: its one established configuration already spans its full 4-actor participating membership.

## Episode comparison

### Singapore check

Singapore has an established coordination tie (direct or hub) in 3 of the 4 Episodes — Bletchley (both the Singapore–Japan dyad and the New Delhi Science Working Group hub), AISI Network (the Joint Testing Exercises hub, in a lead/strand-lead role), and GPAI (both Working Group hubs, including a co-chair role in one) — but *not* Hiroshima, where Singapore's participation does not clear the established-coordination threshold at all. This rules out "the same underlying relationship recurring" as the full explanation, since Singapore's ties span four structurally distinct configurations with different partners and different institutional homes. It is also not simply "no pattern" — Singapore is the only actor holding a *leadership-type* role (co-lead, strand-lead, co-chair) in every established technical or expert-tier configuration it appears in, across three separate Episodes, which is visible directly in the underlying evidence, not inferred from the network alone. The most defensible reading, and the one the network data actually supports, sits between two of the four options the task offered: **repeated involvement in distinct configurations**, with a caveat already flagged in the Cross-Case Matrix (§8) and reproduced here rather than re-discovered — the Bletchley Singapore–Japan dyad and the AISI Network's Joint Testing Exercises hub are explicitly described in the frozen Bletchley Episode itself as conducted "under the AISI network," meaning these two of Singapore's four ties plausibly reflect one underlying institutional relationship observed across two Episode boundaries, not two fully independent data points — a **possible duplicate** in this dataset's terms (both raw observations retained; neither deleted nor merged). This is a claim about how the two raw observations should be *counted*, and it is a separate question from whether the Singapore–Japan raw observation is itself well-corroborated: on that separate question, the Bletchley-era evidence is Singapore's own Research Note, and the AISI Network's later record of the same activity is **cross-Episode corroboration** of it (`corroboration_scope = cross_episode`), not same-Episode corroboration by Japan, whose own Bletchley country-attribution record remains silent throughout — see Dataset 2's Table 2A note and `Coding-Decisions-Log.md`, 2026-09-07. **This is now tagged formally, not only stated in prose:** `Stage5-Dataset2-Established-Coordination.md` tags both rows with the shared identifier `REL-SGP-JPN-AISI-TESTING-01` and a `possible_cross_episode_duplicate: true` flag (added 2026-09-07, researcher-authorised), and that file's "Deduplicated relationship count" note recomputes Singapore's 5 raw observations as 4 distinct underlying relationships on that basis (the "four ties" figure in this memo's own prose, below, already groups Singapore's two GPAI Working-Group raw observations into one descriptive tie — see the dataset note for how the two counts relate). The other two ties (the New Delhi Science Working Group; the GPAI Working Group co-chair role) are institutionally and evidentially independent of that relationship and of each other. **This memo does not infer a general Singaporean structural characteristic from this pattern.** With only 4 Episodes in the study, and Singapore appearing prominently in what may be closer to "2.5 independent cases" than 3 (or, formally, 4 distinct underlying relationships across 3 Episode records rather than 5 raw observations across 3), the finding is reported as a repeated, evidence-grounded pattern within this specific set of four Episodes — not as evidence that Singapore holds this role in middle-power AI coordination generally.

### Institutional-entrepreneurship check

Korea/Bletchley, Japan/Hiroshima, and India/GPAI were kept structurally separate, as instructed, and the network representation shows they are **not** a common relational pattern — they resolve into three different structural outcomes:

- **Korea/Bletchley (Seoul host-authorship) has zero representation in the established coordination network.** Its M finding never clears "plausible, approaching established" in the frozen Episode (`Stage5-Edge-Validation-Table.md` row 6), so no edge — dyadic or hub — was created for it in Dataset 2. It appears only in the participation dataset (as "host") and in the exclusion table. This is the clearest possible way the network representation "keeps separate" a candidate that does not meet the same evidentiary bar as the other two.
- **Japan/Hiroshima (Friends Group creation) is a true singleton-attachment hub** — Japan is the only actor attached to the Hiroshima AI Process Friends Group node in Dataset 2, with no second actor sharing that specific established-M finding.
- **India/GPAI (chairmanship / GPAI–OECD Integrated Partnership) is also a true singleton-attachment hub** — structurally identical in shape to Japan's Friends Group tie (one actor, one hub), though substantively different in kind (India's act integrates two existing institutions; Japan's act constructs a new one from scratch).
- A fourth, related but distinct case surfaced by the network representation itself: **Canada's and Japan's GPAI founding role attaches *two* actors to the same "GPAI Founding Coalition" hub**, with no documented tie between Canada and Japan specifically — they are each individually one of 15 founders, not a documented Canada–Japan pair. This is structurally different again from both Japan's and India's singleton ties, and is not converted into a Canada–Japan coordination edge anywhere in this dataset, consistent with the instruction not to convert unilateral entrepreneurship into coordination edges.

The network representation therefore supports treating "institutional entrepreneurship" as a family of at least three distinct structural forms within this study (singleton attachment; multi-actor attachment with no established inter-actor tie; and — for Korea — no established attachment at all), not as one recurring relational pattern with three interchangeable instances.

## Robustness and caveats

- All metrics beyond simple counts and affiliation degrees are computed on very small graphs (2 edges for the direct actor-to-actor network; 7 hub nodes for the configuration network) — density, betweenness, and component counts are reported because the task requested them, with an explicit caveat, attached at first use, that their comparative weight is limited at this scale.
- The bipartite participation network's high density is an expected, low-information result: participation is deliberately the lowest evidentiary bar in this study's own framework (Codebook §11's Critical Rule exists precisely because participation is common and coordination is rare), so a dense participation network and a sparse coordination network is confirmation that the datasets were built correctly, not a novel finding in itself.
- One counting error from the prior Stage 5 pass was caught and corrected before this stage's metrics were computed: `Stage5-Dataset2-Established-Coordination.md`'s own summary had undercounted Table 2B as 19 rows; the table itself was always correct at 22 rows. All metrics in this memo and in `Stage5B-Network-Metrics-Dataset.md` use the corrected count (24 total established edges: 2 dyadic + 22 hub-based).

## Relationship to the Cross-Case Matrix

The network evidence **confirms** the Matrix's general "participation ≫ coordination" expectation and its identification of AISI Network as the exception to selective sub-coalition formation. It **qualifies** two of the Matrix's findings: (1) the "selective sub-coalition inside a broad forum" pattern, which the network data shows operates differently in GPAI (tier-based selectivity within universal actor-level inclusion) than in Bletchley and Hiroshima (actor-level exclusion of a genuine subset) — a distinction the Matrix's §8 configurations table already contained implicitly but did not state as a contrast; (2) the institutional-entrepreneurship "three recurring cases," which the network representation shows are not equally weighted — Korea's case has no established-coordination representation at all, a sharper statement than the Matrix's own §6 flag of an "unresolved recommendation." Neither qualification requires rewriting the frozen Matrix — both are refinements visible only once the relational structure is represented explicitly, not corrections of an error. **No contradiction between the network evidence and the Cross-Case Matrix was found.** The technical/epistemic capability hypothesis (Matrix §10, "Complicated") is **not materially changed** by this stage — Singapore's network pattern is consistent with, and adds a structural illustration of, the Matrix's existing finding, not new evidence bearing on its classification.

- Whether a future, researcher-approved schema extension should formally support recording that two hub nodes (e.g., the Bletchley-episode joint testing exercise and the AISI Network's own testing-exercise hub) are institutionally the same underlying body observed at two points — currently handled only through a textual note, not a structural link.
