# Network Dataset Validation Memo — Stage 5, First Controlled Stage

## Purpose and scope

This memo documents how the two Stage 5 relational datasets (`Stage5-Dataset1-Participation-Access.md`, `Stage5-Dataset2-Established-Coordination.md`) and the accompanying `Stage5-Edge-Validation-Table.md` were constructed from the four frozen Episodes (`EP-BLETCHLEY-SEQUENCE-2023`, `EP-AISI-NETWORK-2024`, `EP-HIROSHIMA-AI-2024`, `EP-GPAI-2024`), the reviewed `03-Episodes/Stage4-Cross-Case-Matrix.md`, and existing Evidence/Source records. No new substantive research was conducted; every fact used was already present in the frozen Episodes or their linked Evidence/Source records. No network metric (centrality, density, clustering, brokerage) was calculated, and no network graph or visualisation was produced — this stage produces validated datasets only.

## How participation edges were defined

A participation/access record (Dataset 1) was created wherever a frozen Episode's own "Country participation" table, Episode-level process reconstruction, or country-level attribution section documents an actor's membership, endorsement, signature, institutional access, hosting, co-chairing, or other formally documented involvement in a component instrument or process — independent of whether that involvement also satisfies the Codebook §11 Critical Rule for M. Participation records were taken directly from each Episode's own descriptive Table 3 and from titled roles (host, co-chair, lead/co-lead, founding member) stated in the Episode-level reconstruction and country-attribution sections. Where an actor is confirmed absent from an Episode (Brazil and India in the AISI Network; Brazil at full-membership level in Hiroshima), no row was created — absence is preserved exactly as the frozen record states it, consistent with the instruction that participation ≫ coordination is a possible, not an assumed, result.

## How established M edges were defined

An edge was created in Dataset 2 only where the frozen Episode text itself uses the word "established" (not "plausible," "emerging," "approaching established," or any hedged formulation) for a specific, named coordination configuration, and where the underlying passage documents an actual interaction, alignment, exchange, or joint activity with an identifiable output — not mere co-attendance, shared membership, endorsement, or parallel unilateral action. Every candidate — included or excluded — was checked directly against the frozen Episode file's own prose (not against a prior summary, and not against this Matrix's own earlier characterisations) before being included, and the exact status found is recorded in `Stage5-Edge-Validation-Table.md`. Two corrections already made *within* the frozen Episode files themselves (the AISI Network's rejected "Canada–Australia co-lead" claim; GPAI's rejected "six-case bloc" claim) were carried forward as exclusions here, not re-opened or re-litigated.

## How expert/state distinctions were preserved

For GPAI specifically, every Working Group participation edge is coded coordination level **expert/epistemic**, never state/diplomatic, and is explicitly annotated in Dataset 2 that the acting party is a named individual expert, not a government negotiator — this follows directly from the Responsible AI Working Group's own report, which states its members "act with full independence" of their governments (a load-bearing qualification already carried through the frozen Episode's own country-attribution sections and preserved here without dilution). Separately, and kept in an entirely distinct set of edges, Canada's and Japan's GPAI founding/institution-building role and India's 2024 chairmanship/institutional-integration role are coded coordination level **state/diplomatic**, because the frozen record documents these specifically as direct governmental acts (founding, hosting, chairing, institutional design), not as expert-nomination acts. No edge in this dataset converts a Working Group's expert-level participation into a government-to-government tie; where a country participates in both the expert tier and (for Canada, Japan, India) the state tier, both are recorded as separate edges to separate hub nodes, never merged.

## How provenance was maintained

Every edge in Dataset 2, and every row in Dataset 1, carries its Episode ID, Evidence ID, and Source ID, verified directly against the individual Evidence records in `02-Evidence/` (not merely against the summarising prose of the Episode file) before being included. Where the same actor pair or actor–configuration relationship recurs across more than one Episode (no such case was found among the established edges in this pass — every established configuration is Episode-specific), the instruction was to retain Episode-specific provenance rather than collapse it into one undifferentiated relationship; this principle was preserved even though no live case required it this round. Where a specific sub-claim within an otherwise-established edge was not independently re-fetched this round (e.g., the founding-specific portion of Canada's and Japan's GPAI founding-role claims, as distinct from their independently-verified Working Group membership), this is noted explicitly in the relevant Dataset 2 row rather than presented as uniformly strong evidence.

## What relationships were excluded, and why

Twelve candidate relationships were examined and excluded; the full list, with the frozen-record passage and reason for each, is in `Stage5-Edge-Validation-Table.md`. In summary, exclusions fall into three categories: (a) M capped at "plausible" or "emerging" and never reaching "established" (Canada–France; India's Working Group design channel; Japan's forum-linkage; Korea's host-authorship; the Japan–Korea Hiroshima channel); (b) a candidate M claim that was specifically tested and explicitly rejected within the frozen record itself after independent verification (the AISI Network's "Canada–Australia co-lead" claim; GPAI's "six-case middle-power bloc" claim); (c) documented participation, membership, hosting, or co-chairing with no documented joint activity clearing the Codebook §11 Critical Rule (founding membership across all four Episodes generally; Korea's and Japan's founding-meeting presentations in the AISI Network; Canada's and Singapore's Friends Group membership in Hiroshima beyond the specific bilateral/unilateral configurations already included).

## Whether the participation network is structurally broader than the coordination network

Yes, substantially, and this was verified rather than assumed. Dataset 1 records 47 participation rows across the four Episodes; Dataset 2 records 24 established coordination edges (2 dyadic, 22 via hub nodes — corrected 2026-09-06 from an earlier miscount of 21; see Dataset 2's own Network-scope section) — meaning most documented participation does not convert into established coordination. This asymmetry is not evenly distributed: `EP-AISI-NETWORK-2024` shows the tightest ratio (7 participation rows converting into 4 established coordination edges, because its single technical configuration already spans nearly its full founding membership), while `EP-HIROSHIMA-AI-2024` shows the widest (11 participation rows, only 2 established coordination edges — Canada's and Singapore's founding membership does not convert into any coordination edge at all). `EP-BLETCHLEY-SEQUENCE-2023` shows an intermediate pattern: broad six-case participation across twelve collective instruments, but established coordination confined to two specific configurations (Singapore–Japan; the Singapore–India–Canada Science Working Group), with Brazil showing no established coordination at all despite sustained participation. This confirms, rather than assumes, the expected direction of the result — but the specific ratio and its unevenness across Episodes was verified case by case, not presumed in advance.

## Ontology and schema limitations

Several representational choices were required because the current canonical schema (`06-Relational-Data/README.md`, Codebook §24–25) does not natively provide for everything this task asked for. None of these were resolved by silently changing `Nodes.md` or `Edges.md` — both remain exactly as they were before this task, and all new content lives in these four separate, clearly-labelled files instead.

1. **No EPISODE node type.** Codebook §24 defines exactly four node types — STATE, COALITION, MECHANISM, INSTRUMENT — and Codebook §25 separately defines EPISODE as an *object identifier* (`EP-[SHORTNAME]-[YEAR]`) used for traceability, not as a node type in the network sense. Dataset 1 (Actor ↔ Episode participation) is therefore not expressible as a node-to-node edge in the canonical Edges.md schema; it is built here as a standalone table of participation facts keyed by Episode ID, not as a new EPISODE node type added to `Nodes.md`.
2. **No canonical ID convention for COALITION, MECHANISM, or INSTRUMENT nodes.** Codebook §25 defines ID formats only for COUNTRY, SOURCE, EVIDENCE, EPISODE, CLAIM, and MEMO. The seven hub nodes used in Dataset 2 (e.g., "AISI Network Joint Testing Exercises," "GPAI Founding Coalition") are referenced by the same descriptive name already used in the frozen Episodes and in `Stage4-Cross-Case-Matrix.md` — no new ID prefix (e.g., "MECH-001") was invented, since doing so would itself be a schema addition requiring the researcher's prior approval per the README's safeguard.
3. **No coordination-level attribute (state/diplomatic, expert/epistemic, technical-operational, mixed) in the canonical Edges.md schema.** This four-value scale was specified directly by this task's instructions, not previously present in Codebook §24's edge-attribute list. It is recorded only in these two new, separate files, not added as a column to the canonical `Edges.md` register.
4. **No PERSON/individual-expert node type.** Named individual experts (e.g., Brazil's Laercio Aniceto Silva, Singapore's Laurence Liew) are documented by name in the "Actor's documented role" / Note columns of Dataset 2, not as separate formal nodes — the Codebook's object-identifier scheme has no ID format for an individual person. The state (CTY-XXX) remains the formal node in every GPAI Working Group edge; the named individual is preserved as descriptive, traceable detail attached to that edge, consistent with the instruction not to redesign the actor ontology and to document a limitation rather than silently work around it.
5. **Multi-actor configurations represented via hub nodes rather than a complete pairwise graph.** This is a representational choice, not a schema change (explained above and in Dataset 2's own modelling note) — flagged here again because it is the single interpretive decision in this pass most likely to affect a future network-metrics stage (e.g., it deliberately avoids inflating degree/centrality counts for configurations where only joint participation in a shared process, not specific pairwise exchange, is documented).
6. **Non-case participants (Australia, France, Kenya, UK, US) have no canonical actor ID.** These states appear in the underlying evidence for several established configurations (notably the AISI Network's Joint Testing Exercises) but are outside this study's six-case scope and have no `CTY-` identifier. They are named in descriptive text only, not given a fabricated ID or a formal node in either dataset.

## Unresolved ambiguities

- Whether the Singapore–Japan Bletchley joint-testing fact should also be reflected in Japan's own country-attribution section within the frozen Episode file — it is currently established at the Episode-level (primary account) but absent from Japan's own individual record, a gap already flagged in the frozen file's own Uncertainties section and reproduced, not resolved, here.
- Whether the founding-specific (as opposed to Working-Group-membership) portions of Canada's and Japan's GPAI founding-role evidence should be independently re-fetched in a future pass — both are currently well-documented public facts not independently re-verified this round, distinct from the independently-verified Working Group rosters that anchor the rest of each country's GPAI edges.
- Whether a future, researcher-approved extension of the canonical schema should formally add a coordination-level attribute and/or an ID convention for COALITION/MECHANISM/INSTRUMENT nodes, given that this task's requirements could not be fully satisfied within the current canonical `Edges.md` schema without such an extension — this is surfaced as a recommendation, not decided or implemented here.
