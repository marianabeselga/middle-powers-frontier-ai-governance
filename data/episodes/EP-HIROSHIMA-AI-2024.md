### Analytical memo

The Hiroshima AI Process/Friends Group Episode shows a fundamentally different coordination pattern from the other two Episodes processed to date: rather than several roughly-comparable bilateral/trilateral configurations, this Episode is structured around one principal convening actor — Japan, which constructed and expanded the Friends Group itself — plus two bilateral "spoke" configurations of differing strength (an established, substantive Japan–India cooperation channel with concrete deliverables and an explicit Hiroshima commitment; a plausible, thinner Japan–Korea working-level channel), while Canada's and Singapore's participation does not clear the coordination threshold beyond membership. This structure converted into the strongest process-level governance leverage found across all three Episodes processed so far: the OECD Hiroshima AI Process Reporting Framework, through which dozens of major AI developers made formal reporting commitments and were subsequently found, on independent analysis, to have improved their disclosed practices — a documented behavioural effect, not merely a signed declaration, and not attributable to any single one of the five study cases or to Japan alone. Institutionalisation is correspondingly strong (Level 3, with the Reporting Framework itself showing Level-4 characteristics), but this collective effect must be read separately from the country-level attribution layer, where only Japan and India show individually well-evidenced coordination contributions, Korea shows a plausible but thinner one, and Canada and Singapore show none beyond formal membership.
