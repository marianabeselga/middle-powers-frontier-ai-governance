### Analytical memo

The Network's principal coordination configuration is a single, well-evidenced relational structure — the Joint Testing Exercises — in which Singapore's leadership combined with Japan's, Korea's, and Canada's differentiated technical contributions (translation, annotation, validation) to produce a completed, cumulative, cross-lingual AI-safety testing capability, distinct from mere founding-member participation in the Network's Mission Statement or Risk Assessment framework. This coordination converted into institutional and technical governance leverage at the process level — evaluation-methodology consensus, actual cross-lingual testing data, and continued institutional evolution (the Network's 2026 renaming and structured recurring convenings) — rather than into binding regulation or documented developer-behavioural change, and this collective effect is not decomposable to any one founding member's individual contribution. Two candidate coordination claims did not survive scrutiny: Canada's claimed co-leadership of the synthetic-content research agenda is not supported by the instrument's own text (a Network-wide collective product), and Korea's founding-meeting presentation, like its Bletchley host-authorship finding, documents national contribution and self-characterised agenda influence rather than documented negotiation or exchange with a named partner. The Episode is therefore best read as one established, high-confidence relational coordination configuration generating genuine process-level governance leverage (institutional, methodological, and informational), embedded within a broader founding membership that, on its own, does not clear the Codebook's coordination threshold.
