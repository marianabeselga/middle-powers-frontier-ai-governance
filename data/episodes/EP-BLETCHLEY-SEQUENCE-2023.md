## Episode-level process reconstruction (2026-09-04k, expanding the 2026-09-04i collective-GL section)

**How to read this Episode.** This section is the Episode's primary analytical account. The Episode/coordination process — not any single country — is the primary unit of causal analysis. The six "Stage 4 processing — CTY-XXX" sections that follow this one are **country-level attributional records**: they preserve the evidence and coding needed to answer "which country mobilised which capability, participated in or contributed to which mechanism, and can plausibly be credited with which effect" — necessary supporting material, not six independent causal chains to be read in place of, or summed to produce, the account below. Where a country's Stage 4 section is silent on a dimension (e.g., Brazil shows no coordination mechanism), that absence is itself a valid empirical finding about this Episode's actual configuration of actors and mechanisms, not a gap to be filled by inference.

**Methodological principle (applies to this Episode and is intended for reuse in cross-episode comparison; the Codebook itself is not modified — this section applies its existing categories at a different unit of analysis, not a new one).** This study's main outcome of interest is whether middle-power coordination generates **collective governance leverage** — an observable consequence of the coordination process as a whole — not the arithmetic sum of six separately-coded country-level GL findings. The per-country Stage 4 sections below code GL as an **attribution/evidence layer**: they ask, for each country individually, whether the Codebook's four-part attribution test (did the country use its position; is there evidence of the mechanism; did it produce a change; can that change be attributed to the country specifically rather than the collective process) is satisfied. Six negative or partial answers to that country-specific question do **not** imply the coordination process itself produced no governance effect — they may equally mean the effect is real but is a property of the *collective* process, not decomposable into any one participant's individual action. This section assesses that separately, directly, using the same GL1–GL6 categories (§15) applied to the coordinated activity of the process as a whole as the unit of analysis, rather than inferring it from the country-level rows above or below.

The six analytically distinct layers this Episode's processing keeps separate, per the researcher's instruction, are:

1. **Country capability and mobilisation (C, S)** — coded per country, Stage 1 baseline vs. Stage 4 deployment within this Episode.
2. **Coordination mechanism (M)** — coded per specific documented coordinated-activity channel, which may involve one bilateral pair, a named trilateral group, or (in principle) more; never inferred for "the whole episode" as an undifferentiated unit, and never credited to one country alone absent documented interaction (Codebook §11 Critical Rule).
3. **Institutional instrument (I)** — inherently collective/process-level by construction: Table 2's declarations, statements and commitments are multi-signatory instruments, coded once per instrument, not decomposed by country.
4. **Leverage point (L)** — coded per coordinated-activity channel (per Codebook §14), following the same channel-based logic as M, not per country in isolation.
5. **Country-level attribution of governance effects (GL, country rows)** — coded per country in the Stage 4 sections below; a secondary evidentiary layer answering "can this specific consequence be pinned on this specific country," not the Episode's headline finding.
6. **Collective governance effect / collective governance leverage (GL, this section)** — coded once, for the coordination process as a whole, directly from evidence of a process-level consequence; this is the Episode's primary analytical outcome for RQ4.

### Explicit non-aggregation statement

This collective finding is **not** derived by adding, averaging, or otherwise combining the six countries' individual GL codings above (five GL6, one GL1, plus a shared/joint GL1 for three). It is assessed independently, directly from process-level evidence — primarily the New Delhi stage's own documented outputs (SRC-PIB-2026-003/004) and the Seoul-stage Frontier AI Safety Commitments (SRC-UKGOV-2024-003) — exactly as the Codebook's GL definition requires ("observable consequences of coordinated action," §15), with the *coordinated action* here being the multi-year, multi-host Summit sequence itself, not any one country's contribution to it. The relationship between the two layers is genuine and worth stating plainly: **the coordination process produced real, verifiable, positive governance leverage (GL1/GL2/GL3, moderately GL5), even though most of the six countries individually show GL6 on their own primary channel.** This is not a contradiction — it is exactly the distinction the Codebook's four-part attribution test is designed to preserve, and is this Episode's central substantive finding for RQ4.
