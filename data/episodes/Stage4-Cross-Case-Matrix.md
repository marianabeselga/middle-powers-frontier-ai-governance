### 1a. Bletchley episode-wide Institutionalisation assessment (added in this review, 2026-09-06g)

**This resolves the completeness gap this Matrix itself previously flagged.** Bletchley's frozen Episode file (top summary line) states "Institutionalisation level: Not assessed" and gives no episode-wide reasoning; its "Episode-level process reconstruction" section (added 2026-09-04k) likewise never revisits the field. No new research was conducted to produce the assessment below — it is derived entirely from facts already present in the frozen Bletchley record and its linked Evidence/Sources, applying the same conceptual standard used for the other three Episodes (Codebook §13; avoid crediting from instrument count alone; look for a documented mandate/structured framework for Level 3, and recurring operational practice for Level 4).

**Episode-wide assessment: Level 2 — Organised/repeated.** The Bletchley→Seoul→Paris→New Delhi sequence itself, per the frozen record's own "Stage 2 component instruments" table, consists of twelve declarations/statements/commitments produced across four separately-hosted summits (2023–2026), with New Delhi additionally producing seven thematic working groups and twelve deliverables. This matches §13's Level 2 definition ("repeated meetings, working groups, recurring coordination, or organised cooperation") closely. It does not clear Level 3 ("formal agreement, institutional mandate, established framework, or structured arrangement") at the episode-wide level: the frozen record documents no standing secretariat, membership rule, or governing mandate binding the four summits together as a continuing institution — each is a separately-hosted, sequentially-organised event, not a structured standing body. (This mirrors the same distinction already applied within the frozen record itself to Korea's and Japan's *unilateral* coordination acts — presence and repetition are documented, but a standing institutional mandate is not.)

**Narrower component, assessed separately and not blended into the episode-wide score:** the New Delhi Science Working Group, which produced the Network of AI-for-Science Institutions (`EVD-SGP-016`, `EVD-IND-019`, `EVD-CAN-015`), independently shows Level-3 characteristics within its own scope — a named institutional arrangement with stated membership and objectives (per the frozen record's own Episode-level reconstruction, §"Episode-level institutional instruments"). This is recorded as a **narrower institutionalised component**, not evidence that the Bletchley Episode as a whole reaches Level 3 — consistent with the distinction the task instruction requires between episode-wide institutionalisation and narrower institutionalised components within an Episode.

**Provenance note:** this same Level-2 assessment, with the same reasoning, was previously produced in a read-only methodological review of Bletchley (conducted before the AISI Network episode was processed) but was never written into the frozen Episode file, since that review was explicitly read-only. ## 3. Recurring dimensions (analytical possibilities, not a fixed typology)

The following dimensions appear, empirically, to vary across the four Episodes in ways that look analytically useful. They are presented as **candidate** dimensions for the eventual Stage 5 comparison, not as a settled four-type typology — the evidence is examined for each, and several of the dimensions listed in the task prompt as possibilities did **not** turn out to organise the data cleanly; this is noted explicitly rather than forced.

- **Centralised vs. distributed institutional entrepreneurship — appears useful.** Hiroshima shows a single centralised architect (Japan). Bletchley and GPAI show distributed entrepreneurship (multiple actors each responsible for a different configuration or tier: Canada/Japan/India in GPAI; Singapore/Canada/India/Korea in Bletchley). AISI Network shows a hybrid — one configuration, but with an internal leadership/contributor differentiation (Singapore vs. the other three).
- **Bilateral/small-coalition vs. broad multilateral coordination — appears useful, and cuts across, rather than aligning with, Episode boundaries.** Bletchley and Hiroshima are dominated by bilateral/trilateral configurations; AISI Network's established configuration spans roughly 9–10 named countries (4 study cases plus non-case Australia/France/Kenya/UK/US), and GPAI's Working Groups span 17–25+ countries — both markedly broader than Bletchley's/Hiroshima's configurations, though not identical in scale to each other. This is not simply "old vs. new" or "diplomatic vs. technical" — GPAI (broad) and Hiroshima (mostly bilateral) are both closer to the "diplomatic" end of the arena spectrum, while AISI Network (broad) and parts of Bletchley (bilateral, Singapore–Japan) are both closer to the "technical" end. Arena type and coalition breadth do not co-vary neatly.
- **Diplomatic/institutional vs. technical/epistemic coordination — appears useful and is the clearest recurring axis.** AISI Network and GPAI's Working Groups are technical/epistemic in character; Hiroshima and most of Bletchley's configurations (except Singapore–Japan) are diplomatic/institutional. This axis is examined in depth in §5, below.
- **State-led vs. expert-led coordination — appears useful, and is GPAI-specific in how explicitly it is evidenced.** Only GPAI's material contains an explicit, sourced statement that coordination participants "act with full independence" of their governments (`SRC-OECDAI-2025-001`). The other three Episodes' M findings are all coded at the state/institutional level (ministers, officials, named government institutes) or at a technical-institute level where the institute is itself a government body (AISI Network) — none present the epistemic-independence qualification GPAI's Working Groups do. This suggests the state-led/expert-led distinction may be more a property of *this specific institutional form* (GPAI's multistakeholder design) than a general axis running through all four Episodes — flagged as a possible artifact of institutional design rather than a universal dimension, pending Stage 5 confirmation.
- **Selective sub-coalitions within broad forums — appears useful and recurs in exactly the same shape across three of four Episodes.** Bletchley's Singapore–India–Canada Science Working Group, Hiroshima's Japan–India bilateral channel, and GPAI's founding/chairmanship trio (Canada/Japan/India) are all instances of a small, specifically-named subset of actors achieving established coordination within a much larger nominal forum. AISI Network is the exception — its established configuration (Joint Testing Exercises) already comprises all four study-relevant founding members together, not a smaller sub-coalition within them.
- **Degree of institutionalisation — appears useful, but as a tentative pattern only, and the *type/location* of institutionalisation looks more relevant than the *level*.** With Bletchley now assessed (§1a): Level 2 (Bletchley) reaches GL5 at moderate confidence; Level 3–4 (Hiroshima) reaches GL5 established; Level 4 (AISI Network, GPAI) does not reach GL5 at all. Institutionalisation level alone does not track GL5 attainment — if anything, the two *lower*-institutionalisation Episodes in this small set are the ones that reach a behavioural/compliance effect, while the two *highest*-institutionalisation Episodes do not. Examining *why* narrows this further: Bletchley and Hiroshima each institutionalised a **developer-facing** instrument (the Frontier AI Safety/Impact Commitments; the OECD Hiroshima Reporting Framework) that gives GL5 a location to be observed through; AISI Network and GPAI institutionalised **government-institute-facing or expert-facing** instruments only (joint testing reports, Working Group outputs) with no developer-facing channel. This is offered as a **tentative comparative pattern, not a causal claim**, from four cases: it is at least as consistent with "the type/location of an Episode's institutionalised instruments conditions which GL categories are even observable" as with any claim that institutionalisation level itself suppresses or enables GL. Consistent with Codebook §13's own instruction that institutionalisation "should not automatically be treated as governance leverage," and with Decision Rule 13 (absence of evidence is not evidence of absence) — AISI Network's and GPAI's GL4/GL5 "not established" findings reflect an absence of a developer-facing governance location, not a demonstrated absence of any underlying regulatory/behavioural effect.
- **Type of institutional conversion and type of governance leverage produced — both appear useful and are the clearest source of variation among the four.** See the "GL type" row in §1 and the discussion in §4, below.
- **Complementary vs. overlapping capabilities — examined, but the evidence does not cleanly distinguish these as opposed categories across the four Episodes.** Where capability complementarity is established at all (Bletchley's and AISI Network's technical configurations; GPAI's Working Groups), the capabilities involved are consistently complementary (e.g., Singapore's assurance/testing capacity paired with Japan's/Canada's technical capacity, not duplicative of it), not overlapping. No Episode's frozen material documents a case of duplicative/overlapping capability *causing* a coordination failure — this candidate dimension is not disconfirmed, but the material does not provide a contrasting case to test it against, so it is set aside as **not adjudicable on the current evidence**.

## 4. Comparing the breaks

For each Episode, the pathway is examined at three junctures: capability→coordination, coordination→institutional leverage, and leverage→observable governance effect.

**Bletchley.** (1) Capability→coordination: the evidence is consistent with a break specifically for Brazil — diplomatic capacity (C4) was mobilised (sustained escalating representation) but did not convert into any established coordination mechanism; this appears attributable to the absence of a documented joint activity with a named partner, not to a capability deficit (Brazil's C4 was itself rated Strong at Stage 1 and mobilised in-episode). (2) Coordination→institutional leverage: not broken where a configuration is established (Singapore–Japan, Singapore–India–Canada both converted into named instruments). (3) Leverage→observable effect: the pathway is interrupted at the *country* level for most cases (GL6 individually for 5 of 6) but not at the *collective* level, where GL1/GL2/GL3 are established and GL5 reaches moderate confidence. The limitation is best described as **an attribution-scale mismatch** — real governance effects exist, but are properties of the multi-country process rather than any one participant — rather than a coordination or institutionalisation failure.

**AISI Network.** No juncture shows an established break in the collective pathway. The evidence is consistent with the Joint Testing Exercises configuration converting cleanly from mobilised capability through to information/standards/institutional effects. The only limitation is evidentiary: GL4/GL5 are not established because the Episode's own instruments (technical testing/evaluation, not developer-facing commitments) do not create a mechanism through which a regulatory or behavioural effect could, even in principle, be observed within the material reviewed — this is a **scope limitation of the Episode's own instruments**, not a failure of coordination or institutionalisation.

**Hiroshima.** (1) Capability→coordination: the evidence is consistent with a break for Canada and Singapore specifically — both show mobilised C4 (institutional access, participation) that did not convert into an established coordination mechanism beyond Friends Group membership; again, this appears attributable to the absence of documented bilateral/joint activity, not a capability deficit (both cases' Stage 1 C-ratings are Strong). (2) Coordination→institutional leverage: not broken for Japan or the Japan–India configuration. (3) Leverage→observable effect: **not broken at the collective level** — this is the one Episode where the full pathway, including GL5, is empirically established. The main limitation identified is *positional* rather than causal: Korea's bilateral channel with Japan is real but thinner (Hiroshima is one of several fora discussed, not the specific negotiated subject) — the evidence is consistent with position compatibility and institutional access both being present for Korea, with the limiting factor being the *specificity* of documented coordinated activity, not tractability or capability.

**GPAI.** (1) Capability→coordination: not broken — both epistemic (Working Group) and state-level (founding/chairmanship) mobilisation convert into established coordination. (2) Coordination→institutional leverage: not broken — Working Group activity converts into recurring, multi-year institutional output, and the 2024 institutional integration is a genuine leverage-relevant conversion. (3) **Leverage→observable governance effect: this is where GPAI's pathway breaks, explicitly and by the researcher's own repeated acknowledgment.** The evidence is consistent with GPAI generating real institutional and information-production effects (GL1/GL3) without generating regulatory or behavioural effects (GL4/GL5 not established) — the researcher's own material explicitly states that Korea's AI Basic Act and Singapore's domestic framework "cannot be attributed to GPAI alone." The limitation here is best described as **a governance-location limitation**: GPAI's own instruments (declarations, expert reports, tools/portals) do not include a developer-facing compliance or reporting mechanism comparable to Hiroshima's OECD Reporting Framework, so there is no location through which a regulatory/behavioural effect could be observed even if one existed.

**Cross-cutting observation on attributable causes.** Across all four Episodes, where a break was identified, the evidence is consistent with the limitation being located at **coordination** (documented joint activity absent — Brazil in Bletchley; Canada/Singapore in Hiroshima) or at **governance location** (no developer-facing mechanism exists to observe an effect through — AISI Network; GPAI), rather than at capability, capability complementarity, position compatibility, or tractability as such. This is offered cautiously, as a pattern the evidence is *consistent with*, not a confirmed causal finding — the four Episodes do not constitute a large enough or sufficiently varied sample to rule out capability-based explanations definitively, particularly for Brazil (see §5).

## 5. Evidence relevant to the technical/epistemic capability hypothesis

**Hypothesis under examination (not confirmed):** *technical and epistemic capabilities may condition the ability of middle powers to participate substantively in technically demanding coordination configurations.*

**Which actors mobilised C1/C5, and where:**
- **Singapore** mobilised C1/C5 substantively and centrally in the *most* technically demanding configuration identified across all four Episodes (the AISI Network's Joint Testing Exercises; the Bletchley-episode Singapore–Japan testing collaboration) — in both cases occupying a *leadership* role, not merely contributing.
- **Japan, Korea, Canada** mobilised C1/C5 as *contributors* (not leaders) to the same technically demanding AISI Network configuration (dataset translation, annotation, validation) — real, documented, but narrower participation than Singapore's.
- **Brazil, Canada, India, Japan, Korea, Singapore** all show documented C1 mobilisation via named individual experts in GPAI's Working Groups — including Brazil, whose experts are confirmed participants in at least the Innovation & Commercialisation Working Group (`EVD-BRA-021`, independently verified via `SRC-OECDAI-2024-001`).
- **Brazil** shows **no established C1 mobilisation** in Bletchley specifically (Stage 1 PBIA-linked technical capacity was not shown to be deployed within that Episode) and **no established M** in Bletchley at all.

**Was technical/epistemic mobilisation central to the coordination mechanism?** Yes, in AISI Network (the mechanism *is* joint technical testing) and in the Singapore–Japan configuration within Bletchley. It was not central to Hiroshima's established configurations (Japan's Friends Group creation and the Japan–India channel are diplomatic/institutional, not technical) or to GPAI's state-level founding/chairmanship tier (also diplomatic/institutional). GPAI's Working Group tier is technical/epistemic but is explicitly *not* state coordination (§1, above).

**Was the configuration technically demanding?** AISI Network's joint testing (cross-lingual evaluation, benchmark construction) is the most technically demanding configuration identified. Bletchley's Singapore–Japan testing is comparably demanding. GPAI's Working Groups are demanding in a different sense — sustained multi-year expert deliberation rather than a discrete technical exercise. Hiroshima's established configurations are not technically demanding; they are diplomatic/agenda-setting in character.

**Did actors with weaker documented technical/epistemic mobilisation participate differently?** The evidence is consistent with this in one specific comparison: within AISI Network, Canada, Japan, and Korea (contributor-tier technical mobilisation) participated differently from Singapore (leadership-tier) — a real, graded difference correlated with the depth of documented C1/C5 mobilisation. Within Hiroshima, by contrast, the actors with the *weakest* documented technical mobilisation (Canada, Singapore — whose GPAI/AISI-Network technical profiles are otherwise strong) also show the *weakest* coordination — but Hiroshima's own established configurations are not technical in character, so this cannot be read as technical capability conditioning participation in a technical task; it is more consistent with those two actors simply not having pursued a bilateral diplomatic channel with Japan, for reasons this material does not document.

**Does the evidence support, weaken, complicate, or fail to adjudicate the hypothesis?** **Complicates, and partially weakens, rather than confirms it.** The clearest apparent support (Singapore's leadership role tracking its strongest documented C1/C5 profile in the most technical configuration) is genuine but is a single case, replicated in a structurally similar way across two Episodes (Bletchley, AISI Network) that may share underlying causes rather than being independent confirmations. Against this, GPAI directly complicates the hypothesis: Brazil — the case with the weakest documented technical mobilisation in Bletchley — shows real, confirmed, named-individual technical/epistemic participation in GPAI's Working Groups, in a genuinely multilateral expert body. This means Brazil's Bletchley outcome cannot be read as evidence of a general Brazilian technical-capability constraint; it is specific to Bletchley's own configuration structure (a diplomatic summit sequence with few technical joint-activity opportunities), not to Brazil's capability as such. **The hypothesis, as stated, is not adjudicable from four Episodes alone** — it would require either more Episodes or a more granular within-Episode comparison (e.g., why did Brazil not participate in a Bletchley-analogous technical configuration, if one existed and it did not join) to distinguish "capability conditions technical participation" from "the opportunity structure of each Episode differs, and capability is only one of several conditioning factors, likely alongside diplomatic priority-setting, invitation/network access, and the specific technical vs. diplomatic character of the Episode itself."

**Explicit caution on Brazil, per instruction:** this file does **not** conclude that Brazil's limited coordination in Bletchley was caused by insufficient technical capability. The GPAI evidence directly cuts against a capability-deficit reading. The more cautious, evidence-consistent statement is: *Brazil's technical/epistemic capability was not shown to be mobilised within Bletchley specifically, but was shown to be mobilised within GPAI — the difference is at least as plausibly a property of each Episode's own configuration structure and opportunity set as of Brazil's own capability.*

## 7. Preliminary cross-case analytical memo

Across the four Episodes, the most important variation is not in whether coordination occurred — some form of established coordination is found in every Episode — but in **its shape and scale**: Bletchley and Hiroshima are organised around a small number of bilateral or trilateral configurations embedded in a much larger declaratory process, AISI Network is organised around one broad configuration that already includes all study-relevant participants, and GPAI is organised around a genuinely large multilateral expert body operating alongside a small, separate tier of state-level founding and chairmanship acts. No single configuration type recurs identically across all four; what recurs is the *pattern* of a small, specifically-named sub-group achieving demonstrable coordination within a much larger nominal membership — visible in Bletchley's Science Working Group, Hiroshima's Japan–India channel, and GPAI's founding/chairmanship trio, though notably absent from AISI Network, where the established configuration already spans the full study-relevant membership.

What appears to distinguish genuine coordination from mere participation, consistently across all four cases, is the presence of a documented, named, joint activity with an identifiable output — a joint testing exercise, a bilateral joint statement with concrete deliverables, a working group report with named co-authors — rather than co-attendance, endorsement, or shared membership, however extensive. Every Episode contains cases where extensive institutional access and participation did *not* clear this bar (Brazil and, on most channels, Canada in Bletchley; Canada and Singapore in Hiroshima; the six study cases as an undifferentiated bloc in both AISI Network and GPAI), and the Codebook's Critical Rule for M was applied identically in every instance to reach that finding.

Conversion into institutional or governance effects appears facilitated less by the *breadth* of a coordination configuration than by whether it produces a discrete, attributable output — an institution (the Network of AI-for-Science Institutions; GPAI's Expert Support Centres), a methodology (the AISI Network's and Singapore–Japan's joint testing), or a reporting mechanism (the OECD Hiroshima framework). Institutionalisation *level* itself does not appear to predict GL5 attainment across the four cases — with Bletchley now assessed at Level 2 (§1a), the two lowest-institutionalisation Episodes (Bletchley, Hiroshima) are the ones that reach a behavioural/compliance effect, while the two Level-4 Episodes (AISI Network, GPAI) do not. Examined more closely, this tracks the *type* of institutionalised instrument at least as well as the level: Bletchley and Hiroshima each institutionalised a developer-facing commitment or reporting mechanism, while AISI Network and GPAI institutionalised only government-institute- or expert-facing outputs, with no comparable channel through which a developer-facing effect could even be observed. This is offered as a tentative pattern from four cases, not a causal claim — consistent with the Codebook's own caution that institutionalisation should not be treated as governance leverage.

The causal pathways break at different points in different Episodes, and — importantly — the breaks are not evenly distributed across the theorised C→M→I→L→GL sequence. In Bletchley, the pathway is essentially intact at the collective level and breaks only when decomposed to individual countries (an attribution-scale issue, not a process failure). In AISI Network, no break is evident, but this appears to reflect the Episode's own narrow instrumental scope (a technical testing network has no obvious channel to a regulatory or behavioural effect) rather than a coordination limitation. Hiroshima is the only Episode where the full pathway, including a demonstrated behavioural/compliance effect, is empirically complete. GPAI shows the clearest and most explicitly evidenced break, located precisely between institutionalisation and regulatory/behavioural effect — and this is treated here as a genuine, disciplined negative finding rather than a gap to be inferred away, consistent with the researcher's own repeated acknowledgment in the underlying material.

Capability complementarity appears to matter as a *marker* of genuine coordination specifically for the **relational** M configurations — those involving two or more named actors combining distinct capabilities (Singapore–Japan; Singapore–India–Canada; Canada–France; Japan–India; the GPAI and AISI Network Working Groups): in every one of these, where M is established, complementarity is also established, and complementarity is not found established without M. **This does not extend to the unilateral institutional-entrepreneurship type of M finding** — Japan's Hiroshima Friends Group creation, and Canada's/Japan's GPAI founding role — where complementarity is either not applicable in the same sense (a single actor constructing an institution is not a case of two actors' capabilities combining) or established only among a much broader group than the specific M finding concerns (GPAI's 15 founders generally, not Canada-and-Japan specifically). The pattern is therefore narrower than "complementarity tracks M generally" — it holds for the relational subset of M findings across the four Episodes, and this subset/non-subset distinction is itself a finding worth retaining, not a limitation to smooth over.

GPAI adds two things not visible in the other three Episodes. First, it is the only case with a primary source explicitly stating that participating experts "act with full independence" of their governments — surfacing, more clearly than any other Episode, the general methodological distinction between expert/epistemic and state-level coordination that this comparison has had to apply throughout, but that only GPAI's own material states directly. Second — noting that AISI Network shares the same *result* (Level-4 institutionalisation without established GL4/GL5, correcting an earlier overstatement in this memo that GPAI was unique in this) — GPAI is distinctive in *how* that negative finding is evidenced: the researcher's own underlying material repeatedly and explicitly disclaims specific attribution ("cannot be attributed to GPAI alone," for Korea's AI Basic Act and Singapore's domestic framework), making this the clearest case across all four Episodes where the source material itself, not only the coding process, states the limitation. AISI Network and GPAI together, not GPAI alone, demonstrate that durable, well-resourced institutionalisation and demonstrated regulatory/behavioural governance leverage are empirically separable outcomes, not two faces of the same variable.

Several things in this comparison remain hypotheses, not established conclusions: that technical/epistemic capability conditions participation in technically demanding configurations (complicated, not confirmed, by the Brazil/GPAI evidence in §5); that a small named sub-coalition within a larger forum is a generally necessary condition for coordination (consistent with three of four Episodes, but AISI Network's contrary case has not been explained); and that the location of the causal break is best explained by the absence of documented joint activity or of a developer-facing governance location, rather than by capability, complementarity, position compatibility, or tractability as such (offered as a pattern consistent with the evidence, explicitly not as a demonstrated causal claim, given the small number of cases).

**Does broader coordination necessarily produce stronger GL?** No. GPAI's Working Groups (broadest configuration, 17–25+ countries) do not reach GL5; Hiroshima's narrowest established configurations (bilateral, 2 countries) contribute to an Episode that does. **Do selective sub-coalitions inside broad forums recur as a meaningful pattern?** Yes, in three of four Episodes (§8's "reading across configurations" observation), with AISI Network as a genuine, unexplained exception where the established configuration already comprises the full relevant membership rather than a smaller sub-group within it.

## 11. Candidate causal propositions for later testing

Presented as provisional propositions only, each traceable to specific Episode/Evidence material, not as findings. **These are emergent, inductively-generated propositions — formulated from the observed cross-case patterns during this comparative review — and are analytically distinct from the three ex ante analytical expectations that were formulated before the cross-case comparison and are assessed separately, on their own terms, in §12 below (see §12.5 for the methodological distinction between the two categories).**

1. **When a coordination configuration produces a discrete, named joint output (an instrument, institution, or completed technical exercise), it is more likely to show an observable collective GL effect than configurations documented only as ongoing participation or membership.** *Supporting:* all four Episodes (Science WG→GL1; Joint Testing→GL2/GL3; Friends Group/OECD Framework→GL1–GL5; GPAI Working Groups→GL1/GL3). *Complicating:* GPAI's Working Groups do produce a discrete output (reports) yet still do not reach GL4/GL5 — an output is apparently necessary but not sufficient. *Concerns:* the M→I→GL link. *Confidence:* moderate.
2. **Collective governance leverage of the compliance/behavioural type (GL5) appears conditioned on the coordinated process having institutionalised a developer-facing (not only government- or expert-facing) instrument.** *Supporting:* Bletchley (Frontier AI Safety/Impact Commitments), Hiroshima (OECD Reporting Framework) both reach GL5; AISI Network and GPAI, with no developer-facing instrument, do not. *Complicating:* none identified, though the small N means the developer-facing/not distinction may be confounded with the diplomatic/technical arena distinction (§9). *Concerns:* the I→GL link (governance location). *Confidence:* moderate.
3. **A small, specifically-named sub-coalition or single-state entrepreneur operating inside a much larger nominal forum recurs as a common route to established coordination within broad multilateral processes.** *Supporting:* Bletchley (Science WG), Hiroshima (Japan's Friends Group creation; Japan–India), GPAI (India's chairmanship; the founding trio). *Complicating:* AISI Network, where the established configuration already spans the full relevant membership rather than a smaller sub-group. *Concerns:* T/M. *Confidence:* moderate-low — recurs in three of four Episodes, but the fourth's divergence is unexplained by the current material.
4. **Genuine, adjudicable capability complementarity co-occurs specifically with relational (multi-actor) coordination mechanisms, not with unilateral institutional-entrepreneurship acts.** *Supporting:* every relational M finding across all four Episodes. *Complicating:* none found, but this proposition is close to definitional (complementarity presupposes two or more actors) and should be treated as a scope clarification more than a discovered causal pattern. *Concerns:* the T/M relationship. *Confidence:* moderate, with the caveat about its partly definitional character.
5. **Technical/epistemic capability conditions the depth of a case's participation once inside a technically demanding configuration more than it determines whether that case gains access to such a configuration in the first place.** *Supporting:* Singapore's leadership-tier participation in two technical configurations, tracking its strongest documented C1/C5 profile. *Complicating:* Brazil's shift from no documented technical mobilisation (Bletchley) to documented expert participation (GPAI) with no documented capability change, and Canada's/Korea's uniform "contributor" participation in both technical Episodes despite differing diplomatic profiles elsewhere. *Concerns:* the C→M link. *Confidence:* low-moderate — the most hypothesis-like of the five, explicitly flagged as needing more cases or a within-case comparison to adjudicate.

## 12. Assessment of Ex Ante Analytical Expectations

**Purpose and scope.** This section closes out the three analytical expectations that were formulated from the project's theoretical framework *before* the four-Episode comparative analysis was carried out, assessing each against the four frozen Episodes as coded, the reviewed Cross-Case Matrix (§§1–11 above), and existing linked Evidence/Sources where necessary. No new substantive research was conducted for this section, and no frozen Episode, Evidence, or Source record was consulted for facts beyond what is already cited elsewhere in this Matrix. The three expectations are reproduced below **exactly as originally formulated**, without rewriting, strengthening, or retrospective modification. They are assessed here as ex ante analytical expectations, in the qualitative, comparative sense appropriate to a four-case study — not as hypotheses subjected to statistical testing, and not as claims of generalisation, necessity, sufficiency, or causal proof. Throughout, formulations such as "the comparison is consistent with," "the evidence provides tentative support," "the pattern is complicated by," and "the expectation is only partially supported" are used deliberately, in place of stronger causal language.

### 12.1 Proposition 1 — Collective leverage

> *Coordination is more likely to generate governance leverage when participating middle powers can combine resources, capabilities, and positions in ways that increase their collective capacity to influence frontier AI governance.*

**Assessment: Partially supported.**

**Cross-case evidence.** Combination of capabilities is documented, and adjudicable, only where M is coded as a *relational* (multi-actor) configuration — Bletchley's Singapore–Japan and Singapore–India–Canada configurations; AISI Network's Joint Testing Exercises; Hiroshima's Japan–India bilateral; GPAI's Expert Working Groups (§3, §8, §9). In every one of these relational configurations, where capability combination is established, some collective governance leverage (at minimum GL1 and/or GL3) is also established. This is consistent with the proposition's core logic. However, the proposition's implicit scaling claim — that a *greater degree* of combination (more participating middle powers, more capabilities pooled) increases the *collective capacity to influence governance*, and therefore produces *stronger* leverage — is not borne out: GPAI's Working Group configuration, the broadest combination of capabilities and positions across the largest number of countries of any configuration examined (17–25+ countries, all six study cases represented), is also the configuration that fails to reach GL4 or GL5, while Hiroshima's narrowest established configurations (a single bilateral channel, plus one state's unilateral institutional act) belong to the one Episode that reaches every GL category including GL5.

**Supporting Episodes.** Bletchley and AISI Network, where relational combination of complementary capabilities (Singapore's assurance/testing capacity with Japan's/India's/Canada's respective capacities) co-occurs with established GL1–GL3; Hiroshima, where the Japan–India combination of diplomatic/institutional and Global South/market-access capacities contributes to the Episode's collective GL1.

**Complicating Episodes.** GPAI most directly complicates a scaled reading of the proposition: its Working Groups represent the largest, most broadly combined configuration of resources, capabilities, and positions among middle powers of any Episode, yet this scale does not translate into the strongest collective leverage — GPAI's collective GL ceiling (GL1/GL3 established, GL2 plausible) is lower than Hiroshima's, which rests on much narrower combinations. Bletchley also complicates a simple "more middle powers combining = more leverage" reading at the *country* level: five of six study cases show country-level GL6 despite the Episode's own broad, high-position-compatibility membership.

**What the comparison suggests.** The evidence is more consistent with a refined reading than with the proposition's literal, scale-sensitive form: coordination that combines complementary capabilities *within a specific configuration that is converted into a discrete institutional or technical output* is more likely to show observable governance leverage than participation without such combination or conversion — but the *number* of middle powers combining, or the *breadth* of capabilities pooled, does not itself appear to be the operative variable. Distinguishing the elements the researcher specified: **substantive coordination** (M established) is the clearest discriminator between Episodes/configurations that show GL and those that do not; **capability combination** and, where adjudicable, **capability complementarity** track M closely but are not independently observed to scale with GL strength; **position compatibility** is high (declaratory) across nearly every case in every Episode, including cases with no established coordination at all (e.g., Brazil in Bletchley), so it does not discriminate outcomes on its own; **institutional conversion** — whether a configuration produces a discrete, named instrument or output — appears to be the more consequential link between combination and observable leverage than the *number* of participating countries; and **collective GL**, consistent with the finding already recorded in §9, is shown in this dataset to emerge from specific, often narrow configurations rather than from the aggregate participation of all countries in an Episode. This refines, without altering the wording of, Proposition 1: the comparison is consistent with combination-and-conversion mattering, but not with breadth-of-combination being the mechanism.

**Limits of inference.** Four Episodes, several of which share configurations with a genuinely small number of relational M findings, are not sufficient to establish that breadth of combination is *never* consequential — only that it does not show the expected positive association in this specific, small set. Complementarity itself is adjudicable only for relational M configurations (§9), so the proposition cannot be tested at all for the unilateral institutional-entrepreneurship findings (Japan's Hiroshima creation; Korea's and India's host/chairmanship acts), which are a substantial share of this study's established coordination findings. GPAI's failure to reach GL4/GL5 may also be confounded with the governance-location limitation discussed under Proposition 2 below, rather than being solely a function of the *scale* of combination — the two expectations are not fully separable with the current evidence.

### 12.2 Proposition 2 — Institutionalisation

> *Coordination is more likely to generate durable governance leverage when it is translated into institutional instruments or practices that structure behaviour, information, standards, or compliance.*

**Assessment: Partially supported**, with an important qualification the evidence itself surfaces.

**Cross-case evidence.** Comparing the four Episodes directly on institutionalisation level and GL profile (§1, §1a, §3):

| Episode | Episode-wide institutionalisation | GL1 (institutional uptake) | GL2 (standards uptake) | GL3 (information effects) | GL4 (regulatory effects) | GL5 (compliance/behavioural effects) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bletchley | 2 — Organised/repeated (§1a) | Established | Established | Established | Not established | Moderate |
| AISI Network | 4 — Operationalised | Established | Established | Established | Not established | Not established |
| Hiroshima | 3–4 — Formalised approaching Operationalised | Established | Established | Established | Not established | **Established** |
| GPAI | 4 — Operationalised | Established | Plausible | Established | Not established | Not established |

Translation into an institutional instrument or practice is associated, across all four Episodes without exception, with established GL1 and GL3 (institutional uptake and information effects) — every Episode that institutionalised *any* instrument shows these two categories established. This supports the proposition's weaker, more general claim. But the proposition also names durability and the structuring of "behaviour... [and] compliance" — and here, level of institutionalisation does not track outcome: the two Level-4 ("Operationalised") Episodes, the highest level observed, do **not** reach GL4/GL5, while Bletchley (Level 2) reaches GL5 at moderate confidence and Hiroshima (Level 3–4) reaches GL5 as an established finding. This is the same comparative observation already recorded in §3, §7, and §9, restated here specifically against Proposition 2's wording: **institutionalisation level does not track GL strength across the four Episodes, while the type and governance location of the institutionalised instrument (developer-facing, as in Bletchley's Commitments and Hiroshima's OECD Reporting Framework, versus government-institute- or expert-facing, as in AISI Network's and GPAI's outputs) may be more consequential.** This is not converted into a causal claim here, consistent with the instruction and with the Codebook's own caution (§13) that institutionalisation "should not automatically be treated as governance leverage."

**Supporting Episodes.** All four, for the institutional-uptake/information-effects component of the proposition (GL1/GL3 track the presence of an institutional instrument consistently).

**Complicating Episodes.** AISI Network and GPAI complicate the durable/compliance component specifically: both reach the highest institutionalisation level observed (4) without reaching GL4 or GL5, which runs counter to a reading of the proposition in which *more or deeper* institutionalisation produces *more* durable, behaviour-structuring leverage.

**What the comparison suggests.** The comparison is consistent with institutionalisation being a necessary enabling condition for the informational/standards tier of governance leverage, but not sufficient, and not scaled by level, for the compliance/behavioural tier the proposition also names. The qualifying variable the four Episodes suggest — tentatively, and consistent with the framing already developed in §3/§9 — is the *type and governance location* of what gets institutionalised, not the *level* of institutionalisation as such.

**Limits of inference.** The proposition specifically invokes *durable* governance leverage, and none of the four Episodes offers a long observation window: all four are recent (2023–2026), and none of the coded GL findings has been tested for persistence over an extended period. The "durability" component of Proposition 2 is therefore effectively untested by this dataset, independent of the institutionalisation-level finding above — this is a limitation of the available evidence, not a finding about durability itself. In addition, the type-of-instrument variable identified as a candidate qualifier is confounded, in this four-Episode set, with the diplomatic/technical arena distinction discussed in §3 and §9 (developer-facing instruments happen to occur in the two more diplomatically-anchored Episodes) — the current evidence cannot separate "type of instrument" from "type of governance arena" as the operative variable.

### 12.3 Proposition 3 — Structural contingency

> *The effects of coordination depend on the structural and institutional conditions in which it operates, including technological and market dependencies, geopolitical relationships, regulatory and institutional environments, and dependence on external providers of AI technologies and infrastructure.*

**Assessment: Not adjudicable on current evidence**, for most of the specific mechanisms named, with only a partial and weak exception for the two conditions that were coded at all.

**Cross-case evidence.** Of the five structural/institutional conditions named in the proposition — technological dependencies, market dependencies, geopolitical relationships, regulatory/institutional environments, and dependence on external AI technology/infrastructure providers — only two have any documented presence in the four frozen Episodes: geopolitical relationships and regulatory/institutional environments, both captured at the coarse level of Codebook conditions S3 and S4. Per the "Relevant structural conditions" row in §1, S3 and S4 are recorded for every Episode as **contextual, not re-derived** — meaning they were carried forward as background from Stage 1/2 coding and were not independently analysed, within Stage 4's episode-level reconstruction, as variables whose variation was traced through to a specific coordination or governance-leverage outcome. Technological dependencies, market dependencies, and dependence on external AI infrastructure/providers do not appear as coded variables in any of the four Episodes' frozen material at all; the current evidence base was not built to speak to them.

**Supporting Episodes.** None of the four Episodes traces a documented causal mechanism connecting a named structural condition to a specific coordination or governance-leverage outcome, so none can be said to *support* the proposition in the causal sense the wording implies. At most, Hiroshima's Japan-centred, G7-anchored coordination shape and GPAI's OECD-anchored, broad-multilateral-expert shape are each *consistent with* their respective institutional environments being consequential — but this is co-occurrence, not demonstrated effect.

**Complicating Episodes.** Bletchley complicates a straightforward structural reading: all six study cases share substantially the same broad geopolitical/institutional backdrop (the same summit sequence, the same set of hosting states, the same general multilateral safety-governance environment), yet the resulting coordination outcomes vary sharply, from Singapore's multi-configuration leadership to Brazil's complete absence of established coordination. If the shared structural backdrop were doing most of the causal work, this degree of within-Episode variation would be harder to account for; something more specific than the shared structural/geopolitical environment — for instance, bilateral relationship-building or opportunity structure, as already discussed in §9 and §10 — appears to matter at least as much.

**What the comparison suggests.** The four Episodes are consistent with coordination effects varying across different governance arenas and institutional environments in a general sense (the arena-type distinction already central to §3 and §9), but they do not provide within-Episode variation in the *specific* structural conditions the proposition names (technological/market dependency, external-infrastructure reliance) that would allow the proposition to be tested against alternative explanations. The proposition's general spirit — that coordination does not operate in a structural vacuum — is not contradicted by anything in the four Episodes, but it is also not demonstrated by them in the specific, mechanism-level form in which it is stated.

**Limits of inference.** This is the clearest of the three expectations where the evidence is genuinely insufficient rather than merely mixed. Adjudicating Proposition 3 as stated would require case-level coding of technological, market, and infrastructure-dependency conditions that is not currently part of the Stage 1–4 coding scope, and would need to trace a specific structural condition's variation through to a specific coordination or GL outcome within an Episode — neither of which the current material supports. This is stated explicitly, per instruction, rather than inferring a structural mechanism from co-occurrence.

### 12.4 Relationship between Proposition 3 and the technical/epistemic capability finding

The reassessed technical/epistemic capability finding (§10) is one candidate instance of the general logic Proposition 3 describes — technical and epistemic capability could be read as one structural/institutional condition among those the proposition lists (it relates most closely to "regulatory and institutional environments" and, more loosely, to capability-linked technological dependency). §10's own conclusion — *technical and epistemic capabilities may condition the ability of middle powers to participate substantively in technically demanding coordination configurations* — is preserved here exactly as classified, **Complicated**, and is not elevated to an established causal conclusion. The Brazil comparison between Bletchley (no documented technical mobilisation) and GPAI (documented expert participation, `EVD-BRA-021`) is the clearest illustration of why: the same case shows opposite technical-mobilisation outcomes across two Episodes with no documented change in Brazil's own underlying capability, which is at least as consistent with the *governance arena itself* (whether a given Episode offers a technical coordination opportunity at all) conditioning the outcome as with capability being the operative variable. This bears on Proposition 3 only partially and cautiously: it illustrates that *an* institutional/structural condition (the type of governance arena, and whether it structurally admits a technical coordination channel) appears more clearly connected to variation in technical participation than a fixed, case-level capability trait does — which is broadly consistent with Proposition 3's general claim that coordination effects depend on the institutional environment in which coordination occurs. It does **not**, however, establish which structural condition is doing the work, since arena type and capability are confounded rather than independently varied in this four-Episode dataset, and it does not support any claim that technical capability differences by themselves *cause* coordination success or failure.

### 12.5 Ex ante analytical expectations vs. emergent causal propositions — methodological note

This Matrix now contains two analytically distinct categories of propositional content, and they are not to be merged or read as equivalent:

- **Ex ante analytical expectations** (assessed in this §12) are the three propositions — Collective Leverage, Institutionalisation, and Structural Contingency — that were formulated from the project's theoretical framework **before** the cross-case comparison was carried out. They are preserved here in their original wording, unmodified by the comparative findings, and are assessed *against* the four Episodes as a closure exercise, not refined or restated to fit the evidence more comfortably.
- **Emergent causal propositions** (recorded in §11, above) are the five propositions that were generated **from** the observed cross-case patterns during this comparative review itself — inductively derived, not pre-specified, and explicitly labelled provisional and subject to further testing (§11's header note, added in this pass, makes this label explicit).

Keeping these two categories separate matters methodologically: assessing the three ex ante expectations *first*, on their own original terms, and only *then* examining what propositions the comparison itself suggests, guards against retrospective theory-fitting — the risk that a theoretical expectation is quietly reworded, after the fact, to match whatever pattern the data happened to show. Where the two categories overlap in substance (for example, Proposition 2's institutionalisation claim and emergent proposition 2 in §11, both concerning developer-facing instruments and GL5), this Matrix treats the overlap as a point of convergence worth noting, not as license to collapse the ex ante expectation into the emergent proposition's more specific, data-derived form, or vice versa.
