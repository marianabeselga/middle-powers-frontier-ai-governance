**Coordination mechanism (M)**

**Analytical-level statement (2026-09-06e review):** this Episode's M finding operates at two explicitly distinct levels, which must not be conflated: (1) **expert/epistemic coordination** — established at the Working Group level, among named individuals who act independently of their governments; and (2) **state/intergovernmental coordination** — established only for the specific founding, hosting, chairmanship, and institutional-integration acts documented for Canada, Japan, and India. Level (1) is not, by itself, evidence of level (2); a government's act of nominating an expert (a C4 capability act) is not the same as that government negotiating the expert body's substantive output. Both levels are real, established coordination mechanisms in their own right — expert coordination is not downgraded merely for being expert-level, per Codebook §11, which does not require state actors specifically, only "actors" combining/mobilising capabilities toward a governance objective.

One broad, genuinely multilateral configuration is established — the **GPAI Expert Working Groups** — plus **individual founding/institution-building and chairmanship acts** by three of the six cases:

1. **GPAI Expert Working Groups — established (M1/M2).** Independently verified for two of the four Working Groups the Research Note cites (Innovation & Commercialisation; Responsible AI): both show named individual experts from most study cases (5 of 6 for each WG checked) actively participating — in Singapore's case, co-chairing — in bodies whose defined purpose is joint production of recurring reports and tools, evidenced by multi-year (2020–2023) output. This clears the Codebook §11 Critical Rule directly: it is not membership alone, but documented, named, individual-level participation in a joint-production process with an identifiable, recurring output. **Important qualification, load-bearing for country-level attribution:** the Responsible AI Working Group's own report states its members "act with full independence" — i.e., this is expert-level, not government-negotiator-level, coordination. The state's own attributable act is nominating/enabling the expert (C4), which is a materially weaker form of state coordination than a signed intergovernmental joint statement (contrast Bletchley's or Hiroshima's bilateral M findings).
2. **Canada's and Japan's founding/institution-building role — established (M1), individually.** Both co-founded GPAI, hosted early Plenaries/Summits, and established dedicated national Expert Support Centres (CEIMIA; Tokyo ESC) — genuine state-level institution-building acts, not mediated through independent experts.
3. **India's 2024 chairmanship and the GPAI–OECD integration — established (M1/M3a), individually.** A direct, documented governmental act (organising the New Delhi Summit; the resulting institutional merger), not merely expert nomination.
4. **Korea, Singapore (beyond Singapore's WG co-chair role), Brazil — not established** as founding-architect or chairmanship-level contributors within this Episode; their strongest documented contribution is expert-level Working Group participation (configuration 1, above).

### Analytical memo

GPAI presents a genuinely different coordination configuration from the other three episodes processed: not a bilateral or hub-and-spoke diplomatic pattern, but a standing, broad, multi-decade multilateral expert institution in which the six study cases are a minority presence among many members, and in which coordination operates primarily through independent named experts rather than government negotiators. Capabilities were combined and mobilised on two analytically distinct tracks — a small number of state-level founding/chairmanship acts (Canada and Japan's founding architecture and national Expert Support Centres; India's 2024 chairmanship) that are directly state-attributable, and a much broader, genuinely multilateral expert-production process (the Working Groups) in which Brazil, Canada, India, Japan, Korea and Singapore each contributed named individuals, with Singapore additionally occupying a co-chair leadership role. This coordination converted into durable institutional infrastructure — the clearest Level-4 institutionalisation finding among all four episodes, anchored in independently-verified, multi-year recurring Working Group output — and into one major downstream institutional consequence, the 2024 GPAI–OECD Integrated Partnership, which is this Episode's principal collective governance-leverage finding. The chain breaks, however, before reaching regulatory or behavioural governance effects: the researcher's own material repeatedly and explicitly declines to attribute specific national regulatory changes (Korea's AI Basic Act) or developer behaviour to GPAI, and this processing preserves that negative finding rather than overriding it — GPAI generated genuine institutional and epistemic governance leverage, but not the demonstrated regulatory or compliance effects found in `EP-HIROSHIMA-AI-2024`.
