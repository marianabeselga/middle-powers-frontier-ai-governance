## Closed items (no further researcher decision required)

- **S1 (Compute/infrastructure) — FINAL calibration, 2026-09-02: Enabling retained and closed.** New quantified primary-source evidence was added to the Inbox note (Gwangju AI Data Center; Supercomputer No. 6; National AI Computing Center; an OECD competition-policy analysis — formalised as [EVD-KOR-010](../02-Evidence/EVD-KOR-010.md)). This evidence is substantially more specific and quantified than what supported the two prior S1 reviews: 880 H100 GPUs are currently operational at Gwangju (416 government-secured); Supercomputer No. 6 (planned, 8,496 NVIDIA GPUs, 600 petaFLOPS) and a longer-term announced target of 260,000+ NVIDIA GPUs (with Samsung/SK/Hyundai/NAVER co-investment) are explicitly *planned/announced*, not current, capacity, and are not treated as equivalent to it. Dependence is now specific and named (NVIDIA-led AI-semiconductor market, per OECD) rather than general. Weighing this: the dependence concerns chip *sourcing*, not a demonstrated *constraint on access* — Korea is shown successfully securing and rapidly scaling large GPU volumes with substantial government and private funding, distinguishing dependence from lack of access (per the assessment framework applied). Korea's domestic AI-chip design firms (SAPEON, Rebellions, FuriosaAI) exist but are not yet relied upon at scale — the domestic-content mandate for the National AI Computing Center was loosened, not strengthened, a nuance preserved rather than smoothed over. **S1 = Enabling is retained, on stronger evidentiary grounds than either prior review, and Korea's S1 is now closed for Stage 1** unless genuinely new primary evidence later requires reopening it. Korea is not described as technologically autonomous.
- **S1 (Compute/infrastructure) — superseded, prior final evidence-based calibration, 2026-09-02 (same day, prior to the FINAL calibration above): Enabling retained.** Following the controlled revision of Singapore's S1 to Mixed on new primary-source evidence (see `00-Countries/CTY-SGP.md`), Korea's S1 was independently re-verified — not to preserve symmetry, but to test whether Korea's own existing evidence meets the same evidentiary bar. Applying Codebook §16's full definition across four dimensions (domestic infrastructure/compute capacity; access to frontier-relevant compute; external dependence; overall structural significance — see the S1 row above and [EVD-KOR-005](../02-Evidence/EVD-KOR-005.md) for the full breakdown): Korea combines substantial, well-evidenced domestic infrastructure and semiconductor-manufacturing capacity — including a stated ambition for global leadership in AI semiconductors/PIM ([EVD-KOR-001](../02-Evidence/EVD-KOR-001.md)), which positions Korea partly as a hardware *supplier* rather than solely a consumer — with external dependence that is acknowledged but documented only in general, unquantified (OECD/E3-sourced) terms, without a direct government admission of a compute-assembly ceiling or a quantified AI-chip/GPU dependence figure. This is materially different from the evidence that triggered Singapore's revision (a direct, quantified, primary-source government admission of an inability to compete in raw compute assembly). **S1 = Enabling is retained on independent evidentiary grounds, not because Korea has substantial data-centre capacity and not to preserve cross-case symmetry.** Korea is explicitly not characterised as technologically autonomous. **Visible uncertainty, to be carried forward:** the absence of a quantified AI-specific compute/GPU-dependence figure for Korea (unlike Japan's or Singapore's more specific evidence) is a genuine evidentiary gap, not a settled absence of dependence; should more specific primary-source evidence emerge, this rating should be revisited on its own merits.
- **S1 (Compute/infrastructure) — superseded, previously confirmed by researcher decision, 2026-09-02 (same day, prior to the final calibration above):** a controlled Stage 1 consistency review had compared Korea's S1 treatment against Brazil, Canada, India, and Japan, and confirmed Enabling stands, reasoning that each rating faithfully reflects its own Research Note under Codebook §16 (which does not mandate a specific dependence threshold). This reasoning is preserved for the audit trail; it has been superseded by the more detailed four-dimension evidentiary analysis above, which reaches the same conclusion (Enabling) via an independent, evidence-first test rather than a cross-case comparison.
- **Law-in-force distinction (AI Basic Act, Act No. 20676):** a genuinely binding, comprehensive, risk-tiered horizontal AI statute already in force (enforcement date 22 January 2026) — the most comprehensive such statute identified among the five cases processed so far, materially more prescriptive than Japan's Act No. 53 and in a different legal category from Brazil's pending bill and Canada's withdrawn bill. Preserved explicitly in the C2/S4 justifications above. See [EVD-KOR-002](../02-Evidence/EVD-KOR-002.md).
- **OECD source consolidation:** six raw bibliography entries citing three underlying OECD reports (two entries per report, in two cases) were consolidated into three Source records ([SRC-OECD-2024-001](../01-Sources/SRC-OECD-2024-001.md), [SRC-OECD-2025-001](../01-Sources/SRC-OECD-2025-001.md), [SRC-OECD-2025-002](../01-Sources/SRC-OECD-2025-002.md)), per the "consolidate when sources support the same underlying document" principle, rather than registered as six separate sources.

No reinterpretation question arises. See [EVD-KOR-006](../02-Evidence/EVD-KOR-006.md).

## Notes

- Distinguish capability existence from mobilisation (mobilisation is coded at episode level, not here).
- Where evidence is insufficient or conflicting, mark as uncertain — do not infer.
- No aggregate C or S score is calculated, per Codebook §20.
- Institutional/diplomatic membership, convening, and participation (C4) are coded strictly as capability/institutional-access evidence per Codebook §8; they are not treated as evidence of coordination (M) or governance leverage (GL) per the Codebook §11 Critical rule.
- The law-in-force-vs-strategy/soft-law distinction (Codebook-consistent) is preserved throughout: the 2019 National Strategy is a policy roadmap, not legislation; the AI Basic Act (Act No. 20676) is a binding law in force; the amended PIPA is a separate binding Act; the Generative AI Service User Protection Guideline, AI Security Guide, and PIPC guidance documents are non-binding soft law.
