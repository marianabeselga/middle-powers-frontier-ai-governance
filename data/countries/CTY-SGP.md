## Closed items (no further researcher decision required)

- **Controlled Revision (2026-09-02) — S1 revised from Enabling to Mixed.** New primary-source evidence was added to the Inbox note ([SRC-MDDI-2023-001](../01-Sources/SRC-MDDI-2023-001.md), [SRC-MDDI-2024-001](../01-Sources/SRC-MDDI-2024-001.md), [SRC-MDDI-2024-002](../01-Sources/SRC-MDDI-2024-002.md); formalised as [EVD-SGP-012](../02-Evidence/EVD-SGP-012.md)), documenting the Singapore government's own explicit acknowledgment that it "cannot compete with major powers in assembling raw computing power," alongside a S$500 million compute-securing commitment built around external leasing and chipmaker/cloud-provider partnerships. This is direct, primary-source (E1/ES1) evidence tying a structural compute constraint specifically to AI/technological infrastructure — precisely the evidentiary bar the 2026-09-02 consistency review had identified as necessary (and, at that time, absent) to move S1 away from Enabling. With this evidence now present, S1 is reassessed to **Mixed** under Codebook §16's full definition, distinguishing: domestic data-centre/infrastructure capacity (substantial, >1.4 GW, enabling); access to high-performance/frontier-relevant compute (explicitly and directly constrained, per the government's own strategy document); dependence on foreign GPUs/chips and commercial cloud (the anticipated mechanism for meeting future needs, not residual); domestic technological/industrial capacity (genuine and ongoing, via the National Supercomputing Centre and the compute-investment commitment); and constraints on autonomous frontier-compute access (government-acknowledged, not researcher-inferred). This is a substantive reassessment driven by new evidence, not a harmonisation with the other five cases' S1 ratings, nor a rejection of Singapore's genuine domestic infrastructure capacity.
- **Cross-case calibration note (S1, all six cases):** external technological dependence of some form is present in the S1 evidence base for every one of the six cases. S1 is not a binary "dependent vs. autonomous" variable; the relevant analytical question in each case is the *overall configuration* of domestic infrastructure capacity, access to frontier-relevant compute, technological/industrial capacity, and external dependence — not the mere presence of dependence. Different S1 assessments across the six cases (Mixed for Brazil, Canada, India, Japan, and now Singapore; Enabling for Korea) are justified where the *structural significance* of that dependence within each country's own total evidentiary configuration differs, as documented in each country's own Evidence records; this is not a claim that the degree or kind of dependence is comparable across cases.
- **S1 (Compute/infrastructure) — superseded, previously resolved by researcher decision, 2026-09-02: Enabling.** Prior to this controlled revision, a Stage 1 consistency review grounded in Codebook §16 had determined that general macroeconomic/energy-import dependence is not treated as S1 unless the evidence directly establishes a constraint on AI-specific compute, cloud, chips, data-centre capacity, or related technological infrastructure; the IMF evidence in [EVD-SGP-011](../02-Evidence/EVD-SGP-011.md) did not meet that threshold and was retained as background only, leaving S1 = Enabling on the AI-specific infrastructure evidence then available ([EVD-SGP-005](../02-Evidence/EVD-SGP-005.md)). This reasoning is preserved here for the audit trail; it has been **superseded** by the Controlled Revision entry above now that new primary evidence meeting that same threshold has been added.

- **C4 integration judgment, documented not silent:** the Research Note's own C4 rationale hedges at "Moderate/Strong," explicitly naming what additional evidence would justify "Strong" (demonstrated coalition-brokering). Later evidence in the same note-set (Singapore's strand-leadership in the AISI Network's third joint testing exercise) supplies exactly that. Integrated to C-3 (Strong) as a documented judgment call, not a silent upgrade — flagged here for researcher visibility/override. See [EVD-SGP-008](../02-Evidence/EVD-SGP-008.md).
- **S2 out-of-scale label:** Note 1's initial "S2 — Strong" label uses the C-construct scale (Strong/Moderate/Limited) rather than the S-construct scale (Enabling/Constraining/Mixed/Non-salient). Superseded by the same note-set's own fuller, explicitly-reasoned "Enabling" assessment — a documentation artifact, not a standing disagreement. See [EVD-SGP-005](../02-Evidence/EVD-SGP-005.md).
- **Regulatory-model difference (C2/S4), not a coding error:** Singapore has no comprehensive binding horizontal AI statute (unlike Japan's Act No. 53 or Korea's AI Basic Act); its Strong C2/Enabling S4 ratings rest on administrative/institutional capacity and a mature voluntary-framework ecosystem instead. This is preserved as a substantive difference in regulatory model, not smoothed into apparent equivalence with Japan/Korea. Carried into the six-country consistency review. ## Notes

- Distinguish capability existence from mobilisation (mobilisation is coded at episode level, not here).
- Where evidence is insufficient or conflicting, mark as uncertain — do not infer.
- No aggregate C or S score is calculated, per Codebook §20.
- Institutional/diplomatic membership, convening, and leadership of technical exercises (C4) are coded strictly as capability/institutional-access evidence per Codebook §8; they are not treated as evidence of coordination (M) or governance leverage (GL) per the Codebook §11 Critical rule, even where the evidence shows Singapore *leading* rather than merely attending an initiative.
- The law-in-force-vs-strategy/soft-law distinction (Codebook-consistent) is preserved throughout: the NAIS documents are strategy, not legislation; the Model AI Governance Framework and AI Verify are voluntary frameworks; the Elections (Integrity of Online Advertising) (Amendment) Act 2024, the PDPA, and (per its own description) the Workplace Fairness legislation are binding Acts, but none is a comprehensive horizontal AI statute.
