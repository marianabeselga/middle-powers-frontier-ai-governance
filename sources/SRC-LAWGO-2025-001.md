## Notes

**Binding law, now in force** (enforcement date 22 January 2026, prior to today's baseline date). Structurally the most comprehensive of the six binding/near-binding AI statutes examined across the six cases so far: defines "high-impact AI" by a list of high-risk domains (energy, drinking water, health/medical devices, nuclear, biometric/criminal-investigation use, employment/credit decisions, transportation systems, public-service eligibility decisions, student evaluation) (Art. 2(4)); imposes risk-management, transparency/labelling, safety, verification/certification, and impact-assessment obligations on operators of high-impact and generative AI (Arts. 30–35); applies extraterritorially to conduct impacting the domestic market, with a domestic-agent designation requirement for foreign operators (Art. 4, Art. 36); and establishes a statutory AI Safety Institute (Art. 12). This risk-tiered, extraterritorial structure is materially more comprehensive than Japan's Act No. 53 of 2025 (see [SRC-JLT-2025-001](SRC-JLT-2025-001.md)), which explicitly avoids mandatory risk classification or conformity assessment — a key basis for Korea's C2/S4 assessment being rated higher than Japan's.
